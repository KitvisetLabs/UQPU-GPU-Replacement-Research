## Source

Almutlaq et al., “Foundry-Enabled Patterning of Diamond Quantum Microchiplets for Scalable Quantum Photonics,” *Nano Letters* 26(26), 8474–8481; published online 2026-05-07; DOI 10.1021/acs.nanolett.6c01048; related preprint arXiv:2601.20025.

## Reported mechanism

The publisher abstract describes commercial-foundry silicon masks transferred onto diamond by microtransfer printing, with hundreds of diamond quantum microchiplets and controlled coupling to quantum emitters. The modular chiplet approach is reported to support replacement of defective devices and heterogeneous integration. This is a concrete D/G manufacturing route for a specific diamond-photonics platform.

## Blocking unknowns

- Full methods and supplementary data have not been independently reconstructed.
- Quantitative mask/feature dimensions, transfer yield, defect density, optical loss, resonance distributions, emitter-coupling statistics, repeatability and reliability need extraction.
- Cost per qualified chiplet, process throughput, replacement yield, packaging cost and production learning curves are not established by the abstract.
- The authors disclose company-founder interests; provenance and conflict-of-interest context must remain visible.
- Applicability to other materials/modalities (superconducting, silicon-spin, trapped-ion or other photonic systems) is not assumed.

## Unlock criteria

4. Compare with direct-on-diamond patterning under matched device geometry and acceptance thresholds.
