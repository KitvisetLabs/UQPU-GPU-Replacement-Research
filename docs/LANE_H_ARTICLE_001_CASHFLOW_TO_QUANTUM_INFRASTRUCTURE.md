# Lane H Article 001 — From Biomass Cash Flow to Quantum Infrastructure

## Thesis
The Kanusanan Pongpanna Model can be made more testable by treating each industry transition as a gated capital-allocation problem rather than assuming that success in one stage automatically funds the next.

## Stage-gate model

### Gate 1 — Agriculture/biomass -> positive operating cash flow
Required evidence:
- unit economics after logistics, drying, spoilage and working capital;
- repeatable demand;
- automation opportunities;
- positive cash conversion cycle or a financed path to it.

### Gate 2 — Biomass -> higher-value materials
Required evidence:
- conversion yield;
- product specification;
- energy/chemical cost;
- market price;
- quality consistency;
- waste-treatment and regulatory cost.

### Gate 3 — Materials/energy -> electronics/quantum supply chain
Required evidence:
- a real component function that the material can satisfy;
- reliability/purity requirements;
- qualification cost;
- lifecycle cost advantage versus incumbent material.

### Gate 4 — Electronics/quantum -> owned infrastructure
Required evidence:
- cloud-vs-owned crossover;
- sufficient utilization;
- manufacturability;
- stable workload demand;
- financing structure that does not destroy economic advantage.

## Current external signal
Recent 2026 industry developments reinforce the value of manufacturing integration: Quantinuum is pairing quantum-system development with semiconductor/optical manufacturing partnerships and public R&D funding, while IonQ is integrating a foundry-scale manufacturing capability through SkyWater.

This does not validate the Kanusanan Pongpanna Model automatically. It supports the narrower thesis that advanced-computing competitiveness increasingly depends on linking software, devices, manufacturing and capital formation.

## Research questions
1. Which biomass products can realistically reach positive cash flow fastest?
2. Which of those products can feed Lane E/D/G rather than remaining unrelated commodity businesses?
3. What revenue/EBITDA/cash thresholds justify entering semiconductor or quantum manufacturing R&D?
4. Which functions should remain cloud-rented until utilization crosses the ownership threshold?
5. Which public/private funding instruments can finance R&D without forcing premature scale?
