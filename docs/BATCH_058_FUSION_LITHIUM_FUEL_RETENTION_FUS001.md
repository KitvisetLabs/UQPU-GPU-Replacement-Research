## Highest-value delta

A fresh public-source search identified the DOE Office of Science highlight dated 2026-10-15 for the peer-reviewed DIII-D deuterium-retention study by Morbey et al. (*Nuclear Materials and Energy* 43, 101915, 2025; DOI 10.1016/j.nme.2025.101915). The study reports similar deuterium retention across tested pre-lithiated coating thicknesses below lithium's melting point, while lithium-powder injection caused substantial Li–D co-deposition. The authors and DOE summary identify co-deposition and cold-wall accumulation as relevant fuel-retention concerns; liquid-lithium behavior remains a future study.

## FUS-001 / INV-032 integration

The system boundary must eventually include deuterium/tritium retention, recovery, unaccounted fuel, contamination, remote handling, cleanup, component lifetime, availability, recirculating power, and net saleable electricity. A material-level retention improvement is not a net-electricity improvement until matched measurements propagate through a validated plant model with uncertainty.

The study's conditions do not justify extrapolating directly from deuterium on DIII-D coupons to tritium inventory in a commercial fusion plant. The proposed follow-up is FUS-LI-001: freeze source and supplementary-data hashes; reconstruct conditions and uncertainties; preregister matched tests; include fuel mass balance and independent replication; then evaluate whether retention changes are large enough to alter lifecycle cost or availability.
