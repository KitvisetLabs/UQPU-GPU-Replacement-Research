# Literature watch — bio-oil upgrading and accepted-fuel cost boundary

**Date reviewed:** 2026-10-27  
**Primary lane:** E — biomass, materials, energy and infrastructure  
**Evidence/integration owner:** F — reproducibility, energy economics and financing  
**Publication/strategy owner:** H  
**Cross-cutting:** BIO-001, FIN-001, INV-030, INV-031, INV-033–035  
**Evidence class:** peer-reviewed review + peer-reviewed batch experiment; no Pangola reproduction or commercial-cost claim

## Sources and what is genuinely new to this project

1. **Dimitriadis, Tuler and Bezergianni, “Hydrotreatment Upgrading of Hydrothermal Liquefaction Biocrude toward Advanced Fuels: A State-of-the-Art Review.”** ACS Omega, published online 2026-10-01. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1021/acsomega.6c05037. The review surveys upgrading of hydrothermal-liquefaction biocrude toward advanced fuels. Its practical relevance is that the HTL liquid is an intermediate: fuel acceptance and downstream hydrotreatment remain part of the process boundary. It is a review, not a single controlled Pangola experiment or a project-specific cost result.

2. **Jensen et al., “Hydrotreating of Bio-Oil from Updraft Pyrolysis of Straw and Its Miscibility with Ultralow-Sulfur Fuel Oil.”** Energy & Fuels 40(16), 8910–8927; published online 2026-04-08. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.energyfuels.6c00722. The study reports 24 batch experiments using wheat-straw pyrolysis oil and a sulfided NiMo/Al2O3 catalyst, spanning 150–360 °C, 70–130 bar hydrogen pressure, 400–1500 rpm stirring and 2–12 h. The authors report stronger deoxygenation/hydrogenation at higher temperature and hydrogen pressure, and reduced mass-transfer limitation/coke at higher stirring speeds. This is primary experimental evidence for that feedstock and batch apparatus—not for Pangola, continuous scale-up, net energy, or minimum fuel selling price.

The material project delta is not a new fuel breakthrough. It is a sharper accounting gate: crude bio-oil yield is not the accepted-fuel yield, and upgrading severity, hydrogen, catalyst behavior, residence time, utilities and product specification can change both accepted energy and cost. The two studies use different feedstocks/process routes, so their numerical results must not be combined into one synthetic yield or cost.

## Exact UQPU relevance

- **BIO-001 / INV-031:** compare routes on accepted final liquid energy and specification, not only raw condensate or biocrude mass. Include wet/dry feedstock basis, preprocessing, conversion yield, hydrogen, catalysts, utilities, logistics, CAPEX/OPEX, plant uptime and financing.
- **INV-030:** char, aqueous phase, gas and other coproducts need distinct stream identifiers. Credit only independently quantified net coproduct value after incremental separation, upgrading, transport and disposal costs; do not credit the same material twice.
- **FIN-001 / INV-033:** plant capital recovery and pre-commercial R&D capital-lock-up interest are separate ledgers. Lower financed principal lowers absolute interest at unchanged terms; these sources provide no evidence that project WACC or economy-wide rates fall.
- **INV-029:** accepted fuel energy and its full lifecycle cost can eventually feed infrastructure-energy scenarios, but no Data-Center-to-Phone energy or physical-compression claim follows from these fuel studies.
- **INV-034/035 and EQN-001–006:** use measured mass/energy data to generate constrained hypotheses, then test held-out batches and counterexamples. A fitted yield/cost curve remains MODEL_ONLY until its data, assumptions and independent validation are established.

## Limitations and non-claims

Neither source reports a verified Pangola-to-accepted-fuel cost for this project. The straw study is a batch hydrotreatment experiment; the HTL article is a review. Neither establishes complete cradle-to-gate energy closure, commercial uptime, current local Pangola logistics, plant financing terms, a verified coproduct market, or a universally lowest-cost route. No elemental transmutation, commercial ultra-low-cost fuel, new physical law, or economic advantage is claimed.

## Falsifiable follow-up

1. Freeze one Pangola/residue lot ID, sampling protocol, dry-matter/moisture/ash assay and accepted-fuel specification hash.
2. Record raw feed mass and all output streams (liquid, aqueous, char/solids and gas) with calibrated masses and an explicit closure tolerance.
3. For the selected upgrading route, record hydrogen input/consumption, catalyst mass and deactivation/replacement, temperature, pressure, residence time, utility electricity/heat, rejected-product fraction, final liquid mass, density and measured LHV.
4. Run the new batch cost contract with source IDs and no unpriced fields. Keep plant CAPEX recovery separate from R&D lock-up interest; reject overlapping coproduct stream IDs.
5. Compare HTL and pyrolysis only after both have the same accepted-product contract and full cost/energy boundaries. Use independent repeat batches and uncertainty ranges; retain failed and rejected batches.
