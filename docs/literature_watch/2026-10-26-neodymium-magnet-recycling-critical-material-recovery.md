# Literature watch — 2026-10-26: end-of-life NdFeB magnet recovery as a critical-material comparator

**Date recorded:** 2026-10-26  
**Primary owner:** Lane H — literature surveillance/publication  
**Cross-lane consumers:** Lane E (critical-material reduction and recovery), Lane D/G (device and manufacturing material interfaces), Lane F (LCA/TEA/evidence), Lane A (useful-function contracts)  
**Evidence class:** peer-reviewed accepted manuscript; bench electrochemistry + modeled industrial LCA/TEA  
**Promotion:** external material-recovery result only. No UQPU, Pangola-derived substitute, commercial plant-cost, or elemental-transmutation claim is promoted.

## Source and material delta

Wei et al., “Securing critical rare-earth supply through end-of-life NdFeB magnets recycling via molten salt electrolysis with consumable Mg-cathode,” *Nature Communications*, published **2026-10-13** (accepted manuscript ahead of the final version of record), DOI [10.1038/s41467-026-78305-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78305-2). Official source: [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-78305-2).

The authors use end-of-life NdFeB magnet anodes and a consumable magnesium cathode in molten-salt electrolysis at 548–650 °C and 0.75–3 A·cm⁻². They report selective neodymium dissolution, an Mg–Nd liquid alloy, and average current efficiency of 98.4%. Their life-cycle assessment reports 17.36–18.18 kg CO₂-equivalent per kg Nd, approximately 15% of primary-production impact under the study's boundaries. Their techno-economic analysis estimates 90.0 USD/kg Nd for a **modeled 3000 A scale scenario**.

## Genuine novelty and evidence boundary

This is a useful critical-material recovery comparator: it treats the existing magnet as a source of recoverable neodymium and reports laboratory electrochemical performance alongside LCA/TEA scenarios. The distinction between measured cell results and modeled plant economics must remain explicit:

- **Reported experiment:** electrochemical recovery under the stated laboratory operating conditions and feedstock/protocol.
- **Modeled results:** industrial-scale output/cost and lifecycle impact at the 3000 A scenario; these are not a built 3000 A plant's measured production cost or commercial performance.
- **Product scope:** recovery of neodymium into an Mg–Nd alloy is not equivalent to a finished, qualified NdFeB magnet. Subsequent refining, alloy conditioning, magnet manufacturing, quality/yield and recycling-loop losses must be included for a functional comparison.
- **Transfer limits:** the accepted manuscript may be updated before the final version of record; operating conditions, feedstock composition, impurity tolerance, reagent consumption and scale-up need independent verification.

The article does **not** demonstrate that biomass carbon can replace neodymium, does not change elemental identity, and does not show a universal rare-earth recycling route.

## Exact UQPU relevance

- **INV-030 / Lane E:** this is an incumbent recovery/recycling pathway to compare against Pangola/agricultural-residue-derived carbon materials, hybrid composites, reduced critical-material loading, and design-for-recovery. It is not evidence for a biomass-derived substitute. Compare function-to-function: accepted magnet/device performance, lifetime, reliability, and recoverability—not carbon mass against neodymium mass.
- **Lane D/G:** if a UQPU device, actuator, sensor, motor, or manufacturing subsystem uses NdFeB magnets, specify its magnetic, thermal, mechanical, aging and process requirements before considering substitution or recycling. The paper itself establishes no device-specific redesign.
- **Lane F / INV-033:** do not use the modeled 90 USD/kg value as a quote, market price, or financing input without checking the model boundary. Track capital scale, electricity, salt/reagent and magnesium consumption, collection/preprocessing, separation/refining, product yield, environmental controls, maintenance, financing, and time-to-qualified-output.
- **INV-029:** potential material recovery may lower a future system's supply-chain burden, but it does not establish phone-class physical compression or data-center replacement.
- **INV-031/032:** no accepted biomass liquid fuel or net fusion-electricity result follows. Any use of recycled neodymium in energy equipment needs a separate system-level functional and lifecycle analysis.
- **INV-034/035:** no new physical law is proposed. The useful research move is a better measured functional unit and transparent boundary, not an unconstrained materials claim.

## Falsifiable follow-up

1. Reconstruct the paper's mass balance from feed magnet to recovered Mg–Nd alloy, including Nd recovery fraction, impurity partitioning, Mg consumption, electrolyte makeup, off-gas/waste streams, and uncertainty.
2. Reproduce current-efficiency and product-composition results across independent scrap lots and at least one impurity/aging stress case. Record negative runs and rejected product.
3. Separate measured cell electricity/material inputs from modeled 3000 A scale-up. Independently reproduce the LCA/TEA and vary electricity mix, current efficiency, feedstock logistics, magnesium price/consumption, plant utilization, capital cost, and downstream alloy refining.
4. Compare the recovered route with primary Nd and alternative designs on a functional unit such as a qualified magnet meeting a fixed magnetic/thermal/lifetime specification or a device-year of accepted service. Avoid double-counting recycling credits.
5. For Pangola-derived alternatives, preregister the component function and test conductivity/magnetism/thermal behavior, durability, manufacturability, and recovery before claiming substitution. Failure to meet the incumbent function at equal or better lifecycle cost is a negative result, not a near-success.

**Disposition:** relevant published external evidence for critical-material recovery; the plant economics remain modeled and the Pangola substitution question remains open. No project claim is promoted.
