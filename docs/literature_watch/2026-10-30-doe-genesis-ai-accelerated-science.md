## What is genuinely new to this repository

A repository search found no existing record for the September 25, 2026 Office of Science Advisory Committee report. DOE describes an integrated scientific-AI platform connecting high-performance computing, AI, quantum systems and experimental facilities across 33 science and technology challenges. Its highlighted frontiers include AI-accelerated fusion, critical-magnet materials and recycling, and bioeconomy applications.

For fusion, DOE describes AI-supported engineering decisions and real-time prediction as possible ways to manage burning-plasma complexity, with an aspirational U.S. pilot-plant milestone in the 2030–2035 window. For materials, the report proposes AI search across large candidate spaces plus manufacturing-line sensing and recycling to reduce rare-earth supply-chain exposure. These are program priorities and proposed routes, not measured performance gains.

## UQPU relevance and bounded interpretation

- **Lane E / FUS-001:** use the report to identify candidate AI-for-fusion work packages, but retain architecture-neutral accounting for recirculating power, net delivered electricity, availability, component lifetime, maintenance, construction schedule and full financing. The report does not establish net electricity or a fusion cost.
- **Lane E / INV-030:** materials search and recycling are compatible with functional substitution, minimization, recovery and redesign. They do not imply elemental transmutation or prove a candidate material meets device requirements.
- **Lane E / BIO-001 / INV-031:** bioeconomy ambitions can inform future research questions, but no reported claim here validates Pangola conversion, accepted liquid-fuel yield, upgrading cost or commercial fuel price.
- **Lane F / FIN-001 / INV-033:** a faster AI design loop is only a finance benefit if measured evidence shows shorter time-to-evidence, lower financed principal or reduced risk exposure. No WACC or economy-wide rate change follows automatically.
- **Lane A / INV-029:** integration of AI, HPC, quantum systems and facilities is a potential workload/interoperability context, not evidence that a QPU beats classical systems or that remote infrastructure can be hidden inside a phone-class device.
- **Lane H:** the report is relevant to strategic sequencing and funding hypotheses. Strategy remains separate from technical proof and market evidence.

## Limitations

The DOE article summarizes an advisory report; it is not a peer-reviewed controlled experiment, benchmark dataset or end-to-end techno-economic model. Its aspirational milestones and possible AI-enabled outcomes must not be rephrased as delivered capabilities. The full report is linked by DOE's Office of Science advisory-committee index; this watch records only the official public summary reviewed in this cycle.

## Falsifiable follow-up

For each adopted AI-for-fusion, material-search or bioeconomy proposal, create a source-pinned work item that specifies: incumbent baseline; input-data provenance and licenses; target observable; held-out regime; uncertainty and failure cases; independent validation; engineering consequence; and full lifecycle cost. For fusion, compare prediction/control quality and lead time on held-out operating regimes, then quantify consequences for availability, maintenance and net delivered electricity. For material candidates, require independently measured functional properties, manufacturability, durability, recovery and cost per accepted function. For biomass, require feedstock-specific mass/energy closure and cost per accepted MJ of saleable liquid. Do not advance any claim based on the roadmap alone.

## Decision

Index as a strategic research lead for E/F/H. No new physical law, material substitution, commercial fuel, net fusion electricity, quantum advantage, financing-rate effect or Data-Center-to-One-Phone milestone is established.
