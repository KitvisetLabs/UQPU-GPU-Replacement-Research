## Genuine novelty and limits

The crop-straw record reports flash-Joule heating across six residue types and a broad thermal window, with feedstock- and temperature-dependent partitioning among liquid, gas and char. The indexed abstract reports a wheat-straw bio-oil/biogas-favourable intermediate-temperature range, while high-temperature runs shift some feedstocks toward gas and low-temperature runs can favour char. The bamboo article reports a rapid route to nitrogen-doped porous biochar and H2/CO-rich gas, with surface-area/composition results and an emissions estimate conditional on its stated electricity scenario.

The useful delta is **process-intensification and product-selectivity as a hypothesis**, not proof that flash-Joule heating is cheaper, more energy-efficient end-to-end, scalable, or better for Pangola. The available metadata/abstracts do not establish a complete feedstock-to-accepted-liquid mass and energy balance, upgrading requirements, continuous-operation yield, capital cost, or independent replication. The bamboo emissions figure is scenario-dependent and must not be treated as a universal measured lifecycle value.

## Exact UQPU relevance

- **INV-030 / Pangola carbon:** possible route to chars or functional carbon materials; any device-material use still needs composition, electrical/thermal/mechanical performance, durability, contaminant, process-yield and cost-per-good-material tests. No elemental transmutation.
- **INV-031 / BIO-001:** compare cost per accepted delivered liquid-energy/fuel function, not raw condensate yield. Include drying, preprocessing, electricity/pulse-power, reactor duty, condensate water/oxygen, upgrading, hydrogen/catalysts, utilities, CAPEX/OPEX, downtime, logistics, financing and coproduct allocation.
- **Lane D/G:** derive pulse-power, electrode/reactor, thermal containment, metrology, process-control, repeatability and scale-up requirements; no factory/process evidence yet.
- **INV-033 / FIN-001:** include the capital and time required to qualify the route; a faster thermal pulse does not by itself imply shorter project time-to-evidence or lower interest.
- **INV-029:** only downstream if qualified carbon materials or accepted liquid energy measurably reduce the phone-boundary system's total energy, cost or physical burden.

## Falsifiable follow-up

1. Obtain the publisher full text and source data; pin revision, license, feedstock specification, reactor geometry, pulse waveform, measured wall-plug energy, dry-feed basis and complete product mass balance.
2. Recalculate liquid/char/gas yields with uncertainty and energy per kg dry feed; separate gross product yield from accepted fuel yield after upgrading and reject water/oxygen/unstable fractions explicitly.
3. Compare flash-Joule heating with a conventional pyrolysis baseline on matched feedstock, product specification, scale, energy-price assumptions and total CAPEX/OPEX.
4. Test Pangola only after proximate/ultimate analysis, moisture, ash/mineral composition, particle size and feedstock variability are characterized.
5. For char, preregister one target function (e.g. adsorption, electrode, conductive additive or thermal interface), a conventional-material comparator, durability and cost-per-accepted-function gate.
6. Retain null/negative outcomes and independently repeat. No commercial low-cost bio-oil, universal material substitution or net-energy advantage is inferred from the indexed abstracts.
