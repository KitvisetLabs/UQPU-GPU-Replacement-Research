# Literature watch — single-molecule carbene spin–photon interface

**Watch date:** 2026-10-29  
**Primary owner:** Lane D; co-design Lane G; interfaces B/F/H  
**Evidence class:** PEER-REVIEWED EXPERIMENTAL PAPER; DEVICE-LEVEL RESULT; UQPU REPRODUCTION NOT RUN

## Source and date

Roggors et al., “A single-molecule spin–photon interface,” Nature 658, 1153–1159 (2026). Version of record published 2026-10-28; issue date 2026-10-29. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11147-6. Public preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10077.

## Demonstrated result

The study reports a triplet-ground-state carbene molecule in a structurally matched host crystal with single-molecule addressability, spin-selective optical transitions and single-molecule optically detected magnetic resonance. At 4.5 K, reported metrics include optical spectral stability for over an hour, a 38 MHz measured optical linewidth, about 2.2(3) ms dynamical-decoupling coherence and 21(2) ms spin relaxation. Reported photoluminescence quantum yield is 10.8%; the zero-phonon-line fraction is about 19%, or about 2.1% combined emission into that line under the paper's accounting.

The novelty is an experimentally characterized molecular spin–photon interface using bottom-up chemical design and a molecular crystal, broadening the device search space beyond familiar atomic defects.

## Limitations

This is a cryogenic molecular-crystal experiment, not a processor, network node, fault-tolerant device or mass-manufactured chip. Optical linewidth, photon yield, cryogenic requirement, photobleaching, control/readout, integration and packaging burdens must be counted. Solution-grown crystals and drop-cast thin plates do not demonstrate wafer-scale integration, smartphone-display process compatibility, production yield or lower cost. The authors disclose commercial interests in NVision; independent replication remains important. This is not a Pangola-derived material result.
