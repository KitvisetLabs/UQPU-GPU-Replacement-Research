# Literature watch — R&D delay, capital lock-in and continuation bias

**Date checked:** 2026-10-29  
**Primary owner:** Lane H; Lane F FIN-001 evidence and portfolio accounting  
**Evidence class:** PEER-REVIEWED CORPORATE-FINANCE JOURNAL ARTICLE; EXTERNAL DOMAIN; NOT UQPU PROJECT DATA

## Source and date

Marius Guenzel and Tong Liu, “Excess Commitment in R&D,” *The Review of Financial Studies*, 39(7), 2179–2221 (July 2026 issue); published 25 March 2026. Official publisher page: https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/39/7/2179/8540440?login=false. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhag026.

## Genuine novelty

Using pharmaceutical clinical-trial project data, the authors report that delays in completed trial phases were associated with a lower propensity to terminate projects, including analyses instrumenting delays with clinical-trial-site congestion. The paper also reports reduced initiation of subsequent projects, consistent with continuation of delayed work crowding out alternative R&D. This is a portfolio-decision and capital-allocation result—not a finding that all delayed projects should be terminated.

## Evidence class and limitations

This is peer-reviewed evidence from pharmaceutical R&D, not a UQPU experiment. Its external validity to quantum software, device fabrication, biomass conversion or fusion projects is unestablished. Project delays can also convey genuine new information; a fixed kill rule can be as damaging as sunk-cost persistence. The paper does not establish a change in UQPU financing terms, project WACC, central-bank rates or economy-wide interest rates.

## Exact UQPU relevance

- **INV-033 / FIN-001:** make capital lock-up time, cost-to-next-evidence, delay cause, probability of success, remaining future cost, alternative-project opportunity cost and precommitted stop/continue conditions explicit.
- **Lane A:** distinguish delays caused by a fixable software defect from evidence that the workload is unsuitable for QPU execution; don't treat historical effort as a reason to keep an uncompetitive path.
- **Lanes D/E/G:** stage fabrication, material, bio-oil and fusion expenditures behind measurable process-window, accepted-product, net-energy and reproducibility gates.
- **Lane F/H:** compare prospective expected value and future cost rather than sunk cost; preserve the decision rationale and record whether new information justified continuation.

Lowering financed principal directly reduces absolute interest at unchanged terms when that principal would otherwise be financed. The paper motivates better portfolio governance but does not prove that this project can lower its principal, time-to-evidence, risk premium or WACC.

## Falsifiable follow-up

1. Add a FIN-001 decision ledger for each material work package: initial hypothesis, evidence gate, committed and remaining spend, time-to-next-evidence, probability range, failure/continuation criteria, and alternative use of funds.
2. Preregister stop/continue rules based on **future** expected cost and value of information, not past spend; allow documented exceptions when delay yields genuinely new information.
3. Replay historical project decisions, blinded to sunk-cost totals where feasible, and compare decisions against the preregistered rule.
4. Track capital tied up, delay, evidence gained, subsequent-project displacement and cost per resolved uncertainty. Report descriptive results separately from causal claims.
5. Do not infer lower WACC or macroeconomic interest rates without separate financing data and an explicit causal design.
