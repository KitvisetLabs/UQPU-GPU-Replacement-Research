## Primary source and reported result

“Integrated catalytic co-pyrolysis of microalgae-hardwood over CuO/NiO/MoO3-HZSM-5: Bio-oil upgrading and reuse of catalyst-loaded biochar for dye removal,” *Biomass and Bioenergy*, volume 212, article 109296 (2026). DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biombioe.2026.109296.

The article compares in-situ and ex-situ catalytic co-pyrolysis using a CuO/NiO/MoO3-HZSM-5 catalyst system. The publisher-indexed record reports bio-oil yields of 23.9 wt% (in-situ) and 23.5 wt% (ex-situ), an in-situ bio-oil higher heating value (HHV) of 25.27 MJ/kg, and 96.6% methylene-blue removal by the catalyst-loaded biochar under the study's tested conditions.

These are source-reported results for the paper's microalgae/hardwood feedstock, catalyst, apparatus and assay. UQPU has not reproduced the experiment, acquired its raw data, or verified the full mass/energy and lifecycle-cost balance.

## Genuine novelty and why it matters

The useful methodological connection is a coupled process: catalytic upgrading changes liquid composition/energy density, while a spent or catalyst-loaded solid is tested for a secondary adsorbent function. That is a more relevant BIO-001 question than maximizing crude-liquid mass yield alone, because both accepted liquid-energy function and recoverable solid function may influence total process economics.

The study is not directly transferable to Pangola. It uses a different feedstock and a catalyst system containing copper, nickel, molybdenum and zeolite. Those inputs may add critical-material exposure, catalyst replacement cost, regeneration requirements and metal-leaching risks. A single dye-removal percentage does not establish long-cycle adsorbent performance, safe disposal, electronic-material substitution, or a bankable coproduct credit.

## Exact UQPU relevance

- **INV-030 / biomass carbon:** investigate only functional substitution, hybridization, recovery/recycling or redesign. Catalyst-loaded char is not evidence of semiconductor-grade carbon, diamond formation, elemental transmutation, or universal material substitution.
- **INV-031 / BIO-001:** compare the cost per accepted MJ of saleable liquid after upgrading, hydrogen/catalyst use, utilities, logistics, financing and losses—not raw wt% oil yield alone.
- **Lane A / INV-035:** any ANN or surrogate fit must use source-pinned data, chronological holdouts and feedstock/particle-size/reactor-scale holdouts. Symbolic fit or high in-sample R² is not a physical law.
- **Lane C:** report water/volatile content, measured density, sampling, transport, storage and reconstruction/data costs where the process is digitally optimized; no new memory-device result follows.
- **Lanes D/G:** track catalyst manufacture, reactor/separator heat transfer, solids attrition, recovery, regeneration, component lifetime and cost per good operating hour; no factory or device performance was measured by UQPU.
- **Lane F / FIN-001:** count all feedstock/preprocessing, catalyst inventory/replacement, hydrogen, utilities, waste treatment, CAPEX/OPEX, downtime, logistics and financing. Credit char/adsorbent only if accepted, sold or used in a defined service, and never credit the same carbon or emissions benefit twice.
- **Lane H / INV-029:** low-cost accepted energy and recoverable materials may be enabling inputs to the long-horizon mission; this study is not evidence of Data-Center-to-One-Phone compression or lower project WACC.

## Falsifiable follow-up

1. Pin the publisher article version, raw-data/source availability, code, license and any supplementary files before model reproduction.
2. Reconcile dry-feed, moisture and ash bases; close mass and energy balances; measure liquid density and HHV; quantify gas, aqueous, oil and solid fractions.
3. Test the actual accepted product specification and required upgrading/hydrogen/catalyst demand; distinguish pyrolysis-oil grade from finished fuel acceptance.
4. Measure catalyst activity, recovery fraction, regeneration energy, attrition, service life, metal leaching and adsorbent performance over repeated cycles.
5. Compare a Pangola/agricultural-residue feedstock only after characterization and a controlled experiment; do not transplant the paper's yields or economics.
6. Calculate cost per accepted saleable-liquid MJ and any independently realized coproduct service with uncertainty intervals and non-double-counted credits.
7. If a learned model is used, preregister feedstock-family, chronological and scale holdouts, keep failed fits, and compare against simple physics/engineering baselines under equal total resources.
