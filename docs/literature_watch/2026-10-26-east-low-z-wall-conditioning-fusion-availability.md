# Literature watch — EAST low-Z wall conditioning and long-pulse H-mode

**Watch date:** 2026-10-26  
**Source publication date:** 2026-10-21 (accepted manuscript online)  
**Primary owner:** Lane E — materials, energy, cooling and infrastructure  
**Cross-lane interfaces:** FUS-001 (net-electric cost drivers), INV-032, INV-030  
**Evidence class:** Peer-reviewed journal article / accepted manuscript online; experimental tokamak result  
**Status:** External evidence only; not UQPU project evidence and not a power-plant net-electric demonstration

## Primary source

G. Z. Zuo et al., “Application of low-Z materials for enhancing H-mode plasma performance and pulse duration in EAST with full metal wall,” *Nuclear Fusion* (IOP Publishing), accepted manuscript online 2026-10-21. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-4326/aeb707

## What is genuinely new and relevant

The study compares real-time boron (B) and lithium (Li) powder wall conditioning in EAST with a full-metal wall. The publisher's abstract reports:
- continuous boron injection suppressed high-Z impurity accumulation, lowered effective charge, reduced core tungsten concentration by up to approximately 30%, and enabled reproducible 105-second H-mode discharges;
- a combined lithium conditioning case using a 20 g pre-deposited Li coating plus 1 g real-time powder injection supported a reported 1066-second H-mode plasma;
- across the reported statistical comparison, Li conditioning provided about 20% higher energy confinement than B conditioning under comparable plasma conditions.

These are measured plasma-operation results for a particular tokamak and conditioning protocol. They are materially relevant to plasma-facing-material and availability assumptions in fusion plant models, but they do not directly quantify net electricity.

## Limitations and interpretation boundary

1. A 1066-second plasma discharge is not a demonstration of 1066 seconds of net-electric power export.
2. The reported confinement comparison is conditional on the experiment's plasma and wall configuration; it is not a universal Li-over-B ranking for all machines.
3. The abstract alone does not close plant-level balances for injected/retained/recovered Li or B, tritium inventory and retention, erosion/redeposition, tungsten lifetime, remote maintenance, outage time, component replacement, or waste treatment.
4. It does not establish lower fusion electricity cost, higher commercial availability, reduced recirculating power, or an economic optimum. Those require integrated plant and finance models with measured inputs.
5. This source does not support elemental transmutation or any claim that Pangola carbon can replace lithium, tungsten, or boron by chemical identity. INV-030 remains functional substitution/recovery/redesign only.

## Exact UQPU relevance

- **Lane E / FUS-001:** Add wall conditioning and impurity-control as explicit operating/availability and consumable-material drivers, alongside plasma performance, recirculating power, component life, remote handling, replacement and decommissioning.
- **INV-032:** Treat the result as an experimental input to a cost-driver envelope, not proof of ultra-low-cost fusion. Net delivered electricity must still include recirculating power, capacity factor, construction, fuel cycle, materials damage, cooling/conversion, grid interface, financing and decommissioning.
- **INV-030:** The study motivates functional materials and recovery accounting; it does not show Pangola-derived carbon substituting for the studied low-Z conditioning materials.
- **INV-033:** Any financial implication must be calculated only after measuring how the conditioning changes outage time, consumable use, component lifetime and capital lock-up. A plasma-performance result alone cannot justify a lower WACC or financing spread.
- **INV-034/035:** Use the experimental observations as constraints for model refinement; do not treat a fitted plasma model as a new physical law.

## Falsifiable follow-up

1. Retrieve the full manuscript and supplementary/source data; extract discharge-by-discharge plasma parameters, conditioning mass, injection timing, impurity indicators, confinement, pulse duration and uncertainty.
2. Pre-register a matched comparison that controls for plasma current, heating, density, wall state and operating scenario. Test whether the Li-versus-B confinement difference persists on held-out discharges.
3. Close material balances for Li/B deposition, erosion, retention and recovery; quantify tungsten impurity, fuel recycling, tritium-retention implications, maintenance and conditioning-system energy.
4. Feed measured deltas into FUS-001 only as scenario inputs. Report a net-electricity/cost change only if the plant model includes the above consumables, component lifetime, availability, recirculating power and financing.
5. Independently reproduce the extracted data and analysis before changing any project evidence status.

## Decision

**Advance literature knowledge; do not advance commercial-evidence status.** This result justifies a better measured wall-conditioning/availability term in FUS-001. The next gate is a reproducible matched-discharge dataset and material/maintenance balance, followed by plant-level net-electric accounting.
