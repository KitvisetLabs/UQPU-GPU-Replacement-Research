# Literature watch — waste-biomass pyrolysis techno-economic baseline

**Access date:** 2026-10-29  
**Primary source:** Geetanjali Yadav and Patrick Lamers, “Process Feasibility Analysis of Waste Biomass Valorization to Biochar and Bio-Oil via Slow and Fast Pyrolysis,” *Energy & Fuels* 40(9), 4670–4682 (2026), DOI: https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.energyfuels.5c05655  
**Official laboratory metadata/abstract:** https://research-hub.nlr.gov/en/publications/process-feasibility-analysis-of-waste-biomass-valorization-to-bio-2/  
**Evidence class:** PEER-REVIEWED PAPER METADATA + ABSTRACT / UQPU REPRODUCTION NOT PERFORMED  
**Primary owner:** Lane E — biomass/bio-oil; Lane F economics; Lane H provenance

## Genuine delta

The National Laboratory of the Rockies record identifies a peer-reviewed techno-economic process-modeling study that includes preprocessing, slow and fast pyrolysis, and product separation into bio-oil, biochar and syngas hydrocarbons. The abstract reports modeled minimum selling prices of **US$188–260 per tonne for slow-pyrolysis biochar** and **US$6.49–9.68 per gasoline-gallon-equivalent (GGE) for fast-pyrolysis bio-oil**, described by the authors as approximately twice conventional-fuel benchmarks. The abstract identifies feedstock cost, product yield and scale as major sensitivities and says biochar carbon-credit/high-value-use scenarios can improve modeled economics.

The useful UQPU delta is a grounded external baseline and a reminder that low-cost raw biomass does not automatically imply low-cost accepted liquid fuel. Yield, scale, feedstock logistics and product qualification can dominate.

## Evidence boundary and limitations

- The values are source-reported **minimum-selling-price model outputs**, not observed universal market prices, project measurements or guaranteed selling prices.
- Only the official laboratory metadata/abstract was reviewed here; the full paper, model workbook/code, input tables, financial assumptions, uncertainty propagation and supplementary data were not independently audited or reproduced.
- The study is not demonstrated to use Pangola grass. Transfer to Pangola requires feedstock-specific moisture, ash/alkali, composition, yield, preprocessing and logistics data.
- The reported bio-oil is not automatically finished gasoline, diesel, jet fuel or a refinery-accepted intermediate. Upgrading, hydrogen, catalysts, stabilization, water/oxygen content, utilities, CAPEX/OPEX, uptime, storage/distribution and financing must be included for the accepted product.
- Biochar or syngas coproduct credits must be separately saleable, lifecycle-consistent and counted only once. Carbon-credit assumptions are not guaranteed cash revenue.
- The reported dollar basis and assumptions must be recovered from the full article before comparison with present prices or another TEA.

## Exact project relevance

- **INV-031 / BIO-001:** use as a literature-only reference range for the next accepted-liquid-energy cost model; do not copy the MSP directly into a Pangola business case.
- **INV-030:** biochar is a candidate carbon stream, not evidence of electronics/semiconductor critical-material substitution. Material performance and lifetime need separate qualification.
- **INV-032 / FUS-001:** process integration, electricity and heat are relevant cost drivers, but this paper provides no fusion evidence or net-electricity result.
- **INV-033 / FIN-001:** a full TEA must expose financed principal, construction/ramp-up duration, working capital, interest during construction and time-to-evidence separately from WACC or economy-wide rates.
- **INV-029:** any contribution to phone-class infrastructure remains indirect until a measurable service, energy and lifecycle-cost chain is established.
- **Lanes D/G:** the modeled process requires scale-up and product separation; equipment, yield, uptime, maintenance and cost-per-good-output remain separate engineering gates.
- **Lane H:** treat the reported range as a scenario/reference, not as promised cash flow for the staged strategic plan.

## Falsifiable follow-up

1. Pin the full article, supplements/data and exact cost-year/financial assumptions; record hashes and license/access conditions.
2. Reconstruct the published slow/fast pyrolysis TEA with independent unit checks before changing any input.
3. Add Pangola-specific moisture, ash, alkali and composition measurements; use a matched feedstock-preprocessing and product-acceptance contract.
4. Compare pathways on cost per accepted GGE or refinery-qualified energy unit, including conversion yield, upgrading/hydrogen/catalysts, utilities, CAPEX/OPEX, uptime, logistics, financing and uncertainty.
5. Show biochar/syngas credits as separately measured scenarios with no double counting; report results with and without credits.
6. Preserve failed yield/quality cases and sensitivity ranges. If the source model cannot be reconstructed, keep the paper as a literature lead only.

**Decision:** add a source-pinned BIO-001 follow-up gap. No commercial low-cost bio-oil, Pangola transfer, carbon-material substitution, fusion electricity, lower WACC or mission-level advantage is established.
