## Executive delta

Two 2026 peer-reviewed studies not yet indexed in the repository add useful, but limited, comparators for BIO-001 and FIN-001:

1. A 2026 *Energy & Fuels* study compares one-step and two-step supercritical-water liquefaction (SCWL) across biomass groups differentiated by lipid, carbohydrate and protein composition. ## 1. Two-step supercritical-water liquefaction across biomass compositions

**Source:** “Marked Improvement in Bio-Oil Yield and Compositional Quality Using Two-Step Supercritical Water Liquefaction of Different Biomass Types,” *Energy & Fuels* 40(29), 15858–15879 (2026); published online 2026-07-08; issue publication 2026-07-23.  
**DOI / publisher:** https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.energyfuels.6c01592  
**Publisher metadata:** https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.energyfuels.6c01592  
**Publisher-stated license:** CC BY 4.0.

### Genuine novelty

The study compares direct one-step SCWL with a staged process:
- subcritical-water pretreatment with a 30-minute holding time;
- subsequent supercritical-water liquefaction;
- multiple biomass types grouped by lipid-rich, carbohydrate-rich and protein-rich composition.

This is relevant because Pangola grass and agricultural residues are compositionally heterogeneous. A staged hydrothermal path may change fractionation, hydrolysis, condensation and downstream upgrading requirements relative to a one-step reference.

### Limitations and exact BIO-001 relevance

- The retrieved publisher abstract does not expose the full tables, supplementary mass/energy balances or detailed cost data. No numerical yield or cost is transcribed here.
- The paper is not identified as a Pangola-specific study. Biomass composition, ash, moisture, lignin/cellulose/hemicellulose and minerals must be measured for each Pangola batch.
- Bio-oil yield/composition is not accepted fuel function. Final liquid quality, hydrogen demand, catalyst life, heteroatom removal, distillation/fractionation, water recycle, char/gas handling, utilities and full CAPEX/OPEX remain mandatory.
- No commercial ultra-low-cost fuel conclusion follows from a process-condition comparison.

**Falsifiable follow-up:** obtain the full article/supplement and reconstruct its feedstock/procedure/mass balance; then run matched Pangola batches through one-step and two-step routes with identical feedstock preparation, reactor geometry, residence-time definitions, temperature ramps, catalyst loading and downstream upgrading. Pre-register total mass and energy closure, liquid yield on dry ash-free feed basis, accepted-fuel yield/quality, hydrogen/catalyst consumption, water recycle, char/gas energy, uncertainty and lifecycle cost per accepted MJ. Include a one-step control and independent batch replication.

## 2. R&D-intensive firms and credit-supply/monetary shocks

**Source:** Pragati Priya, “The innovation channel of monetary policy and credit supply shocks on firm investment: Evidence from R&D-intensive firms in India,” *Emerging Markets Review* (2026), article 101479, published July 2026.  
**DOI / publisher:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ememar.2026.101479  
**Publisher metadata:** Elsevier ScienceDirect article S1566014126000439.

### Genuine novelty

The paper studies Indian firm-level data over 2002–2024 using panel regressions and local-projection impulse responses. The abstract reports stronger sensitivity of R&D-intensive firms to both monetary tightening and bank credit-supply shocks than for non-R&D-intensive firms, with effects extending beyond investment to broader financial performance measures.

This makes credit availability a separate FIN-001 stress variable rather than treating the policy rate as the only financing shock.

### Limitations and exact FIN-001 relevance

- Full text and coefficient tables were not retrievable in this cycle. No numerical elasticity or confidence interval is quoted.
- The sample is India, not Thailand, and the estimates are not project-specific financing terms.
- R&D intensity is not a direct proxy for UQPU project risk, collateral, lender covenants, currency exposure, stage-gate quality or funding availability.
- The study does not establish a reduction in project WACC or economy-wide interest rates.

**Falsifiable follow-up:** extend FIN-001's tranche ledger with two separately parameterized shocks: (i) interest-rate/refinancing shock and (ii) credit-availability shock (reduced limits, delayed drawdown, covenant tightening or loss of a lender). For each milestone, record required principal, drawdown timing, working capital, maturity, grace period, fees, collateral, probability of delay, cash burn and evidence acceptance. Compare the same milestone plan under matched rate-only, credit-only and joint shocks. Report absolute interest and principal at unchanged terms separately from any hypothesized risk-premium/WACC change.
