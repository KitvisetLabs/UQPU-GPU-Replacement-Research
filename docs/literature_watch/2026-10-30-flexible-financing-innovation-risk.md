## Genuine contribution

The paper develops an intertemporal model with asymmetric information, R&D risk and adverse selection. It models bank lending, equity and hybrid financing as choices conditioned on asset composition, cash flow, innovation risk and institutional quality. The reported model implications are:

- collateralized bank lending can be better aligned with lower-risk innovation, while expected liquidation losses can make debt less suitable for high-risk R&D;
- equity can share radical-innovation risk, but the model requires sufficient institutional quality for investor participation;
- hybrid structures can expand the financing-feasibility frontier when collateral or institutional constraints bind;
- in the model, R&D subsidies can relax financing constraints more effectively than risk guarantees without weakening screening incentives.

The useful novelty for FIN-001 is an explicit, testable financing-instrument choice layer. It complements, rather than replaces, the September 2026 Banco de España working paper on the distinction between internal hurdle rates and financial cost of capital, already indexed in Batch 140.

## Limitations and evidence boundary

The institutional record describes an intertemporal model and its testable predictions. The full 27-page version was linked as open access, but its PDF could not be retrieved in this surveillance pass; detailed equations, calibration, identification and sensitivity were therefore not independently audited. The model's conditional results are not a universal debt/equity ranking. No UQPU tranche, collateral, cashflow, lender term, equity return, subsidy, guarantee, hurdle rate, WACC or interest payment was measured.

The paper does **not** demonstrate lower interest rates, lower project WACC, reduced innovation risk, lower economy-wide rates, or improved UQPU financing outcomes.

## Exact project relevance

- **INV-033 / FIN-001:** distinguish financed principal and debt interest from equity dilution/required return, internal hurdle rate, financial cost of capital/WACC, project/innovation risk premium, guarantees, subsidies and capital lock-up time.
- **Lane H:** financing design for future industrial stages should be linked to milestone evidence and risk rather than assuming a single capital instrument.
- **INV-029–032:** any financing comparison must use the same accepted useful service, material/fuel/electricity boundary, lifecycle CAPEX/OPEX and time-to-evidence; the paper supplies no technical cost reduction.
- **A–G integration:** capital structure can affect how a validated computing/material/energy milestone is financed, but cannot improve its physical or computational performance by itself.

## Falsifiable follow-up

1. Extend the FIN-001 project ledger with instrument type (debt/equity/hybrid), amount requested/committed/drawn/outstanding, coupon/fees/repayment, equity dilution/required return, collateral/asset composition, guarantees/subsidies, cashflow and institutional context.
2. Record project-specific R&D/technical risk, downside and failure outcomes, adverse-selection/screening assumptions, stage gates and dated time-to-evidence.
3. For identical milestone cashflows and technical-risk assumptions, compare feasible debt, equity and hybrid structures; report principal, absolute interest, fees, equity cost/dilution, guarantee/subsidy exposure, lock-up duration and total financing cost per independently accepted milestone.
4. Test predicted instrument choice against held-out actual financing events and preserve failed or rejected projects; do not select only surviving firms.
5. Keep a principal-only counterfactual separate from coupon/spread, WACC, internal hurdle and risk-premium changes. Estimate each from actual records before generalizing.
