## Objective

Determine whether the published slow/fast-pyrolysis cost model can be reconstructed and then adapted to Pangola under an accepted liquid-energy/fuel contract without transferring unverified feedstock, yield, product-quality, coproduct or finance assumptions.

## Current source-reported baseline

The official National Laboratory of the Rockies record identifies the work as a peer-reviewed *Energy & Fuels* article (2026; 40(9), 4670–4682). Its abstract reports modeled minimum selling prices of US$188–260/t for slow-pyrolysis biochar and US$6.49–9.68/GGE for fast-pyrolysis bio-oil, approximately twice conventional fuel benchmarks in the authors' comparison. Feedstock cost, product yield and scale are named as major cost drivers. The full paper and source data were not audited in this cycle; the figures are external model outputs, not UQPU evidence.

## Missing evidence

- Exact cost year, financial parameters, scale, yield assumptions, equipment sizing, operating hours and uncertainty model.
- Reproducible model/data/supplement and independent unit reconciliation.
- Pangola-specific composition, moisture/ash/alkali, collection, preprocessing, seasonal variation and delivered-feedstock cost.
- Accepted product specification and measured liquid yield, water/oxygen content, stability, heating value and upgrading requirements.
- Hydrogen/catalyst, utilities, CAPEX/OPEX, uptime, maintenance, logistics and financing inputs.
- Saleable biochar/syngas credit basis, lifecycle boundaries and non-double-counting controls.
- Independent replication and a matched conventional-fuel/refinery-intermediate comparison.

## Unlock criteria

1. Pin the paper/supplement and reconstruct the published TEA before transferring assumptions.
2. Freeze Pangola feedstock and accepted-liquid product specifications.
3. Build mass and energy balances for preprocessing, conversion, upgrading, product separation and utilities.
4. Calculate cost per accepted GGE/energy unit with low/base/high ranges and uncertainty; report all credit-free and coproduct-credit cases.
5. Separate financed principal, construction/ramp-up time, interest and capital-at-risk from WACC/risk-premium hypotheses.
6. Compare against a matched incumbent process and retain failed quality/yield cases.

## Non-claims

No accepted Pangola bio-oil, commercial ultra-low-cost fuel, material substitution, net fusion electricity, lower WACC or Data-Center-to-One-Phone result is established.
