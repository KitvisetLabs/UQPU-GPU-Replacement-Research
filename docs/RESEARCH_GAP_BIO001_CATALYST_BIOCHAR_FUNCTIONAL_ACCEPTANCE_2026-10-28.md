# Research gap RG-BIO001-CATALYST-BIOCHAR-001 — functional acceptance of catalytic co-pyrolysis products

**Opened:** 2026-10-28  
**Primary owner:** Lane E — biomass/materials/energy  
**Evidence owner:** Lane F — product acceptance, mass/energy and lifecycle economics  
**Method interface:** Lane A — temporal/group holdouts and model validation  
**Status:** OPEN / LITERATURE_ONLY / NO PANGOLA TRANSFER

**Related:** INV-030/031/033/035, BIO-001, FIN-001, Batch 104

## Objective

Determine whether catalytic co-pyrolysis with a recoverable catalyst-loaded char can improve the **accepted saleable-liquid energy function** and a separately accepted solid coproduct enough to lower full lifecycle cost for a characterized agricultural-residue feedstock, without hiding catalyst, hydrogen, regeneration, waste, financing or product-quality costs.

## Source-reported starting point

The 2026 *Biomass and Bioenergy* article reports microalgae/hardwood co-pyrolysis over a CuO/NiO/MoO3-HZSM-5 catalyst system, with 23.9 wt% in-situ and 23.5 wt% ex-situ bio-oil yields, 25.27 MJ/kg HHV for the reported in-situ oil, and 96.6% methylene-blue removal by catalyst-loaded biochar in the tested assay. These are third-party article results. UQPU has not reproduced the experiment, verified raw data, or mapped it to Pangola.

## Blocking unknowns

- Feedstock, moisture, ash, reactor and catalyst differ from the Pangola/residue route.
- Liquid mass yield, volumetric throughput and HHV cannot be mixed without a common dry-feed basis and measured density.
- Crude pyrolysis-oil acceptance is not equivalent to finished transport-fuel acceptance.
- Catalyst purchase, metal loading/loss, regeneration, replacement, hydrogen, utilities, downtime, waste treatment and CAPEX/OPEX are uncalibrated.
- One adsorption assay does not establish repeat-cycle service, safe disposal, leaching limits or a realizable coproduct price.
- Any char/adsorbent/emissions credit may be counted only once and only for a defined, accepted service.
- A surrogate fit cannot replace physical balance closure, held-out feedstock/scale testing or independent replication.

## Required acceptance gate

1. Pin the article, supplementary data, code, license and acquisition hashes where available.
2. Characterize the candidate feedstock on a declared dry-mass/moisture/ash/elemental basis.
3. Close mass and energy balances across liquid, gas, aqueous and solid streams, with measurement uncertainty.
4. Measure liquid density, HHV, stability/quality and the actual acceptance/upgrading pathway; compute accepted MJ rather than crude-oil mass alone.
5. Measure catalyst recovery, regeneration energy, activity retention, attrition, metal leaching and component lifetime across repeated cycles.
6. Validate the solid coproduct's service performance, disposal and market/onsite use independently; forbid duplicate credits.
7. Produce an auditable cost interval including feedstock/preprocessing, yield, upgrading, hydrogen/catalysts, utilities, CAPEX/OPEX, logistics, working capital, interest, financing and risk.
8. For data-driven models, freeze source revision and record hashes, use chronological plus feedstock-family and scale holdouts, compare with simple baselines, and retain failed searches.
9. Require independent replication before any evidence promotion.

## Explicit nonclaims

This gap is not a Pangola experiment, accepted commercial fuel, critical-material substitution result, carbon-negative lifecycle result, fusion-electricity result, WACC reduction, or new physical law. No elemental transmutation is proposed. It does not authorize a paid QPU job.
