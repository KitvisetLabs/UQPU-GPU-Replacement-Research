# Biomass-to-Critical-Materials Strategy

> **Permanent strategic pillar:** see the prominent root-level directive `01_PANGOLA_BIOMASS_CARBON_TECHNOLOGY_STRATEGY.md` and INV-030.

## Strategic objective

Use Pangola grass and diverse agricultural residues as low-cost renewable feedstocks for advanced-material supply chains supporting UQCS, semiconductors, photonics, quantum systems, batteries, data centers, AI/NPU/GPU infrastructure, energy systems, robotics, aerospace and future infrastructure.

A particularly important pathway is **carbon powder / char / advanced-carbon production associated with biomass thermochemical conversion and bio-oil production, upgrading and distillation/refining where scientifically applicable**. These carbonaceous materials are to be purified and engineered into porous carbon, activated carbon, hard/graphitic carbon, films, coatings, fibers, composites, membranes and future nanostructured carbon systems.

The strategic purpose is to reduce total system cost and dependence on expensive or supply-constrained materials, explicitly including **Au, Cu, Ag, Li, Co, Ni, rare-earth elements and other high-value/critical minerals**.

## Scientific boundary

Ordinary chemical, biological, pyrolytic or bio-oil-processing routes cannot turn carbon-rich biomass into elemental gold, copper, silver, lithium, cobalt, nickel, rare-earth elements or arbitrary other elements.

Therefore the research program uses five physically valid strategies:

1. **Functional substitution** — replace the expensive material's required function with a biomass-derived carbon/material where properties permit.
2. **Material minimization** — redesign devices so much less critical mineral is required.
3. **Recovery/recycling** — use biomass-derived sorbents, membranes, carbons or reagents to recover valuable elements already present in waste/process streams.
4. **Hybrid materials** — combine small amounts of critical material with low-cost biomass-derived matrices, coatings or structures.
5. **Architecture/chemistry substitution** — redesign the device or system so the critical material's original function is no longer required in the same form.

## Product pathways

### Carbon family
Biomass -> drying/preprocessing -> pyrolysis/thermochemical conversion -> bio-oil + solid carbonaceous char + process gas -> separation/purification -> activation/graphitization/doping/nanostructuring -> advanced carbon products.

Bio-oil may also undergo upgrading/distillation/refining. Carbonaceous residues or co-products from those operations may be investigated when they are compositionally and economically useful. The project does not assume that bio-oil distillation itself is always the primary route to high-quality carbon powder; feedstock/process characterization decides the route.

Possible functions:
- adsorption/recovery
- battery/supercapacitor electrodes
- conductive additives
- conductive coatings/interconnect substitutes where qualified
- EMI shielding
- thermal interfaces/composites
- catalyst supports
- membranes
- structural/lightweight composites
- recovery/recycling media

Higher-severity processing may investigate hard carbon, graphitic carbon, graphene-like structures, CNT-like structures and other advanced carbon architectures where evidence supports process feasibility and economics.

### Cellulose/lignin family
Biomass fractionation -> cellulose/nanocellulose/lignin -> films, composites, binders, resins, carbon-fiber precursors, membranes and structural materials.

### Mineral recovery from biomass and waste
Some agricultural residues contain useful inorganic fractions. Recover silica/silicon-containing fractions or other naturally present elements only where feedstock composition and purification economics support it.

Biomass-derived carbons may also be engineered to recover Au/Cu/Ag/Li/Co/Ni/rare-earths or other valuable materials from e-waste, industrial wastewater, mining/process streams and recycling systems. This is recovery of elements already present, not elemental transmutation.

## UQCS material-function map

| Conventional dependency | Function | Biomass-carbon research route |
|---|---|---|
| Graphite/carbon | electrode/conductive material | hard carbon, activated/graphitic biomass carbon |
| Cu/Ag/Au | electrical conduction | graphitic/nanostructured carbon and conductive composites for qualified functions; metal-carbon hybrids and metal-loading reduction where direct substitution is insufficient |
| Au/Ag/Pd/Pt-like high-value systems | contacts/catalysis | minimize loading; carbon-supported nanoparticles; alternative catalyst architectures; recover metals from waste streams |
| Li | ion-carrier role in Li chemistry | carbon cannot replace Li ion directly; investigate alternative battery chemistries plus biomass hard-carbon hosts to reduce lithium dependence at system level |
| Co/Ni | cathode/catalyst/alloy functions | alternative chemistry, lower loading, carbon-supported active materials and recovery/recycling |
| Rare-earth materials | magnetic/optical functions | architecture/chemistry elimination or minimization; rare-earth-free alternatives; biomass-carbon recovery media; carbon cannot chemically create rare-earth elements |
| High-purity polymers | insulation/packaging | cellulose/lignin/bio-resin composites where thermal/purity requirements permit |
| Thermal materials | heat spreading/interfaces | graphitic-carbon-rich composites; benchmark thermal conductivity and interface resistance |
| Activated carbon | adsorption/filtration | direct biomass-derived route |
| Battery carbon | anode/conductive function | hard carbon/porous carbon pathways |
| EMI shielding | electromagnetic attenuation | conductive porous/graphitic carbon coatings and composites |
| Data-center / device structural materials | panels/insulation/composites | bio-based composites and carbon materials |

## Required benchmark fields

Every candidate must record:
- target incumbent element/material and exact required function
- feedstock and delivered cost
- moisture/ash/elemental composition
- preprocessing
- process temperature/pressure
- chemical inputs and recovery
- carbon/material mass yield
- energy input
- purification/de-ashing requirements
- electrical/thermal/mechanical/electrochemical/magnetic/optical/adsorption metrics as applicable
- incumbent material/function threshold
- remaining critical-mineral loading
- component-level performance
- contamination/process-compatibility limits
- reliability/cycle life
- CAPEX/OPEX
- waste/emissions treatment
- recyclability/recovery
- TRL/evidence level
- cost per delivered functional unit
- UQCS/system cost, mass, volume and energy impact

## Cost target

The 100× to >=100,000,000× project target remains a system-level research objective. No biomass-carbon pathway is credited with an advantage until measured or defensibly modeled against the incumbent function.

This materials pillar is explicitly linked to the ultimate `00_ULTIMATE_NORTH_STAR_DATA_CENTER_TO_PHONE.md` mission: reducing critical-material dependence, thermal burden, packaging cost and manufacturing complexity is part of making radical physical/economic computing compression possible.
