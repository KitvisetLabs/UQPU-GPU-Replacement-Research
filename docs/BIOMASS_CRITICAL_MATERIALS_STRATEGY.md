# Biomass-to-Critical-Materials Strategy

## Strategic objective

Use Pangola grass and diverse agricultural residues as low-cost renewable feedstocks for advanced-material supply chains supporting UQCS, semiconductors, photonics, data centers, energy systems, robotics and future infrastructure.

The objective is to reduce total system cost and dependence on expensive or supply-constrained materials.

## Scientific boundary

Ordinary chemical or biological processing cannot turn carbon-rich biomass into elemental gold, copper, silver, rare earth elements or arbitrary other elements.

Therefore the research program uses four physically valid strategies:

1. **Functional substitution** — replace the expensive material's function with a biomass-derived material where properties permit.
2. **Material minimization** — redesign devices so much less of the critical mineral is required.
3. **Recovery/recycling** — use biomass-derived sorbents, membranes, carbons or reagents to recover valuable elements already present in waste/process streams.
4. **Hybrid materials** — combine small amounts of critical material with low-cost biomass-derived matrices, coatings or structures.

## Feedstock families

- Pangola grass
- rice husk/straw
- sugarcane bagasse
- cassava residues
- corn residues
- coconut shells/husks
- palm residues
- wood/sawdust
- bamboo
- lignin/cellulose-rich process waste
- other locally abundant agricultural residues

## Product pathways

### Carbon family
Biomass -> drying -> pyrolysis -> biochar -> activation/purification -> porous/activated carbon.

Possible functions:
- adsorption/recovery
- electrodes
- conductive additives
- EMI shielding
- thermal interfaces/composites
- catalyst supports

Higher-severity processing may investigate hard carbon, graphitic carbon and advanced carbon structures where evidence supports the process economics.

### Cellulose/lignin family
Biomass fractionation -> cellulose/nanocellulose/lignin -> films, composites, binders, resins, carbon-fiber precursors, membranes and structural materials.

### Mineral recovery from biomass
Some agricultural residues contain useful inorganic fractions. Recover silica/silicon-containing fractions or other naturally present elements only where feedstock composition and purification economics support it.

This is recovery of elements already present, not elemental transmutation.

## UQCS material-function map

| Conventional dependency | Function | Biomass research route |
|---|---|---|
| Graphite/carbon | electrode/conductive material | hard carbon, activated/graphitic biomass carbon |
| Cu/Ag/Au | electrical conduction | conductive carbon composites for suitable non-critical-current functions; reduce metal loading rather than assume universal replacement |
| Au/Ag/Pd | contacts/catalysis | minimize loading; carbon-supported nanoparticles; recover metals from e-waste |
| Rare-earth materials | magnetic/optical functions | architectural elimination/minimization first; biomass cannot chemically create rare-earth elements |
| High-purity polymers | insulation/packaging | cellulose/lignin/bio-resin composites where thermal/purity requirements permit |
| Thermal materials | heat spreading/interfaces | carbon-rich composites; benchmark thermal conductivity and interface resistance |
| Activated carbon | adsorption/filtration | direct biomass-derived route |
| Battery carbon | anode/conductive function | hard carbon/porous carbon pathways |
| Data-center structural materials | panels/insulation/composites | bio-based composites and carbon materials |

## Required benchmark fields

Every candidate must record:
- feedstock and delivered cost
- moisture/ash/composition
- preprocessing
- process temperature/pressure
- chemical inputs and recovery
- mass yield
- energy input
- purification requirements
- product property metrics
- incumbent material/function
- remaining critical-mineral loading
- CAPEX/OPEX
- waste/emissions treatment
- recyclability
- TRL/evidence level
- cost per functional unit
- UQCS/system cost impact

## Cost target

The 100× to 100,000,000× project target remains a system-level research objective. No biomass pathway is credited with an advantage until measured or defensibly modeled against the incumbent function.
