# Research Gap RG-036 — lithium fuel retention to net-electricity and lifecycle economics

**Date:** 2026-10-25  
**Primary lane:** E — materials, energy, cooling and infrastructure  
**Integration lane:** F — evidence and economics  
**Related invariant:** INV-032; supporting finance link INV-033  
**Status:** DATA_BLOCKED / MODEL_ONLY UNTIL SYSTEM PROPAGATION  
**Source lead:** Morbey et al., *Nuclear Materials and Energy* 43, 101915 (2025), DOI 10.1016/j.nme.2025.101915; DOE Office of Science highlight published 2026-10-15.

## Objective

Determine whether lithium-related fuel retention and co-deposit formation materially change the fuel-cycle cost, maintenance, availability or net delivered electricity of a future fusion plant under a specified architecture.

## Current evidence

The peer-reviewed DIII-D study reports similar deuterium retention across tested pre-lithiated coating thicknesses below lithium's melting point, while in-situ lithium powder injection produced substantial Li–D co-deposition. It is a bounded experiment, not a commercial reactor measurement. The DOE highlight notes that future work will study co-deposited lithium/fuel in liquid-lithium conditions.

## Blockers and limitations

- No reactor-scale tritium inventory/recovery balance.
- No demonstrated extrapolation from deuterium coupons and DIII-D exposures to a commercial fusion plant.
- Liquid-lithium behavior remains a follow-up.
- No validated relation from retention to cleanup, downtime, component lifetime, remote handling, recirculating power or net saleable electricity.
- No calibrated capital, operating-cost, financing or uncertainty propagation.

## Required contract and falsifier

FUS-LI-001 must freeze the primary paper/supplementary-data hashes; record plasma conditions, deposition method, sample location, temperature, coating thickness, co-deposit mass, isotope retention, uncertainty and repetitions; and preregister matched tests with mass balance and independent replication.

The proposed plant-level route is falsified if the retention effect does not reproduce under matched conditions, is not distinguishable from measurement uncertainty, or is too small relative to other validated lifecycle losses to change the net-electricity/cost envelope.

## Unlock criteria

1. Independently reconstructed source-data table and analysis.
2. Matched, repeated retention/recovery measurements with uncertainty.
3. Evidence on liquid-lithium and tritium-relevant conditions before isotope extrapolation.
4. Validated system model linking retained/recovered fuel to inventory, maintenance, availability and net saleable electricity.
5. Complete lifecycle cost boundary and sensitivity/uncertainty analysis.

Until these criteria pass, keep the result as a literature/evidence lead. Do not claim cheap fusion electricity, net-energy gain, commercial LCOE, lower WACC, or a new physical law.
