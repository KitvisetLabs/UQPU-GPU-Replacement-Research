## Question

Can lithium surface retention/redeposition and plasma contamination be jointly controlled under reactor-relevant heat fluxes, while preserving stable plasma operation and reducing the full lifecycle cost of net delivered electricity?

## Current evidence

The peer-reviewed HIDRA study reports model-referenced (R_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx0.91\) to unity across tested hydrogen/helium plasma cases, but also reports high-temperature lithium release that rapidly made the plasma lithium-dominated and prevented sustained operation in vapor-shielding-relevant conditions. The authors state that HIDRA heat fluxes were orders of magnitude below those in reactor-relevant divertors and do not claim that full vapor shielding was established.

## Required acceptance gate

- Pin source and supplementary data; reconstruct the mass-balance calculation with uncertainty.
- Keep gross evaporation, net loss, redeposition/recycling, surface conditioning, plasma impurity, temperature locking and diagnostic limits as separate variables.
- Run matched, preregistered tests with independent diagnostics and a capture/control intervention.
- Demonstrate stable plasma operation and improved net loss without a compensating impurity, cleanup, downtime or maintenance penalty.
- Propagate replicated distributions through fuel inventory/recovery, component lifetime, availability, recirculating power, remote handling and net saleable electricity.
- Use fixed financing terms for absolute-interest sensitivity; any WACC/risk-premium claim requires separate evidence.
