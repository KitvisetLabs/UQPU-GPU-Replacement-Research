# PERMANENT STRATEGIC PILLAR — Pangola & Biomass Carbon for Critical-Material Substitution

## Mission

A central long-term strategy of the UQPU/UQCS program is to build an innovation platform around **carbon-rich powders, chars and advanced carbon materials derived from Pangola grass and agricultural residues**, including carbonaceous streams created through biomass thermochemical conversion and, where applicable, bio-oil upgrading/distillation/refining.

The objective is to reduce dependence on expensive, supply-constrained or geopolitically sensitive minerals/materials across the technology industry by using biomass-derived carbon innovation to:

1. **functionally substitute** the incumbent material where carbon can deliver the required function;
2. **minimize** the amount of expensive material required through architecture, composites or coatings;
3. **recover/recycle** valuable elements using porous carbon, membranes, sorbents, catalyst supports or electrochemical media;
4. **hybridize** small quantities of high-value material with low-cost carbon matrices;
5. **redesign the technology or chemistry** so a critical element is no longer required for that application.

This strategic pillar is intended to support electronics, semiconductors, photonics, quantum hardware, batteries, energy systems, AI/NPU/GPU infrastructure, data centers, robotics, aerospace, advanced manufacturing, future cities and the project's ultimate **Data-Center-to-One-Phone** North Star.

## Priority incumbent materials

The research program explicitly includes the following high-value/critical material families:

- **Gold (Au)** — contacts, bonding, corrosion-resistant conduction, specialized electronics;
- **Copper (Cu)** — wiring, interconnects, busbars, thermal/electrical conduction;
- **Silver (Ag)** — high-conductivity contacts, pastes, coatings and specialized electronics;
- **Lithium (Li)** — electrochemical energy-storage chemistries;
- **Cobalt (Co)** — battery cathodes, alloys and specialized materials;
- **Nickel (Ni)** — battery cathodes, plating, alloys and catalysts;
- **Rare-earth elements** — permanent magnets, phosphors, optical and specialized electronic functions;
- **other expensive/critical minerals and metals** used in semiconductor, quantum, photonic, battery, energy, robotics, aerospace and data-center supply chains.

The list is deliberately expandable as new critical-material bottlenecks appear.

## Scientific boundary

This project does **not** claim that carbon atoms can be chemically converted into Au, Cu, Ag, Li, Co, Ni or rare-earth atoms. Ordinary chemistry, pyrolysis, bio-oil distillation and carbon-material processing do not perform elemental transmutation.

The research target is **functional substitution or architectural elimination**, not elemental imitation.

A candidate is successful only if it satisfies the same required application-level function at acceptable quality, lifetime and system cost. If a metal's unique electronic, electrochemical, magnetic, optical or catalytic function cannot be delivered by carbon, that direct substitution is recorded as unsuitable and the program moves to minimization, recovery, hybridization or an alternative architecture/chemistry.

## Feedstock-to-material platform

Potential research chain:

```text
Pangola grass / rice residues / bagasse / cassava residues / corn residues
/ coconut / palm / bamboo / wood residues / lignin-rich waste / other biomass
        -> preprocessing / drying / fractionation
        -> pyrolysis or other thermochemical conversion
        -> bio-oil + solid char/carbon + process gas
        -> bio-oil upgrading/distillation/refining where useful
        -> collect/select carbonaceous solid or residue streams
        -> purification / de-ashing / activation
        -> graphitization / doping / pore engineering / nanostructuring
        -> powders / fibers / films / coatings / composites / membranes
        -> device/component integration
        -> functional-unit qualification
        -> lifecycle cost + reliability + recyclability assessment
```

The exact route must be selected from measured feedstock chemistry and target-product requirements; this diagram is a research architecture, not a claim that every step is required or commercially viable.

## Functional substitution matrix

| Incumbent material family | Incumbent function to challenge | Biomass-carbon research direction | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Au / Ag / Cu | electrical conduction, contacts, interconnects | graphitic carbon, graphene/CNT-like structures where feasible, conductive composites, printed carbon conductors, metal-carbon hybrids, reduced metal loading | must benchmark resistivity/contact resistance, current density, reliability, oxidation/corrosion and process compatibility |
| Au / Ag / Pt/Pd-like costly catalyst systems | catalytic/contact surface | high-surface-area porous carbon supports, heteroatom-doped carbons, reduced noble-metal loading, recovery media | carbon support is not automatically catalyst replacement; reaction-specific activity/selectivity/lifetime required |
| Li | electrochemical ion carrier | change battery chemistry where feasible; biomass hard carbon/porous carbon as electrode host; explore sodium/potassium/other systems to reduce lithium dependence | carbon does not replace Li ion inside a lithium-ion chemistry merely by being an anode material |
| Co / Ni | cathode chemistry, catalysts, alloys | chemistry substitution, lower-loading electrodes, carbon-supported active materials, alternative battery architectures | must compare energy density, cycle life, safety, power and cost |
| Rare earths | magnetic/optical/phosphor functions | rare-earth-free architecture, reduced rare-earth loading, carbon structural/thermal components, recovery sorbents/membranes | direct carbon replacement of high-performance permanent-magnet/optical functions is not assumed |
| Graphite / synthetic carbon | anode, conductor, thermal/structural roles | biomass-derived hard carbon, activated carbon, graphitic carbon and composites | purification, ash, defect structure, conductivity and lifecycle qualification required |
| Thermal-interface/high-value heat-spreading materials | thermal management | graphitic carbon films/powders/composites, porous carbon structures | compare thermal conductivity, interface resistance, stability and manufacturability |
| EMI/shielding materials | electromagnetic attenuation | conductive porous/graphitic carbon composites and coatings | compare shielding effectiveness over frequency and thickness/mass |
| High-purity polymer/composite materials | packaging, structural/insulation functions | cellulose/lignin-derived carbon/composites/bioresins where compatible | semiconductor/quantum contamination limits must be met |
| Metal-recovery systems | capture Au/Cu/Ag/Li/Co/Ni/REE from waste/process streams | activated carbon, functionalized porous carbon, membranes and sorbents | recovery means extracting existing elements, never creating them |

## Technology-industry scope

The strategy is intentionally cross-industry. Candidate applications include:

- semiconductor packaging, substrates, interposers and thermal management;
- chip/module interconnect and conductive coatings where electrical requirements permit;
- quantum-control packaging and EMI/thermal structures;
- photonic packaging and optoelectronic support materials;
- battery anodes, conductive additives, electrode scaffolds and alternative-chemistry architectures;
- supercapacitors and electrochemical storage;
- catalysts and catalyst supports;
- data-center cooling/thermal/structural materials;
- NPU/GPU/CPU accelerator modules;
- robotics and electric-drive systems;
- power electronics and renewable-energy systems;
- aerospace lightweight conductive/thermal composites;
- filtration, water treatment and critical-mineral recovery;
- recycling and circular material flows;
- future phone-class UQPU/UQCS packaging and energy systems.

## Required research metrics

Every proposed substitution must record at least:

- target incumbent material and exact required function;
- incumbent property/performance threshold;
- biomass feedstock source and composition;
- carbon yield and purification yield;
- ash/metal/heteroatom contamination;
- pore structure / surface area where relevant;
- electrical, thermal, mechanical, electrochemical, magnetic/optical or adsorption properties as applicable;
- process temperature, pressure, chemicals and energy input;
- required activation/graphitization/doping/coating steps;
- component-level performance;
- reliability, cycle life and environmental stability;
- remaining critical-material loading;
- recyclability and recovery route;
- CAPEX/OPEX and cost per **delivered functional unit**;
- system-level effect on UQPU/UQCS cost, mass, volume, energy and manufacturability;
- evidence level and provenance.

Raw feedstock price per kilogram is never sufficient proof of technology advantage.

## Research ladder

```text
Feedstock characterization
-> reproducible carbon precursor
-> purification / property control
-> material coupon
-> incumbent functional-unit comparison
-> component prototype
-> reliability / contamination qualification
-> manufacturable process window
-> pilot-scale economics
-> qualified substitution / minimization / recovery route
-> integration into UQPU/UQCS and wider technology industry
```

At any stage, a negative result is retained. A failed direct substitution can still reveal a valuable minimization, recycling, catalyst-support or architecture-elimination route.

## Relation to the Ultimate North Star

The project's highest mission is the eventual compression of extreme data-center-scale useful capability into one affordable phone-class device. That requires not only computational breakthroughs but also drastic reductions in:

- expensive material content;
- supply-chain dependence;
- packaging mass/volume;
- thermal-management burden;
- energy-system cost;
- manufacturing CAPEX/OPEX;
- maintenance and recycling cost.

For that reason, Pangola/agricultural-biomass carbon is not treated as a side sustainability project. It is a **central materials-and-economics strategy** that must be co-designed with quantum computing, semiconductor/photonics, NPU/GPU/CPU replacement, memory/storage, energy/cooling, manufacturing equipment and the Kanusanan Pongpanna strategic-finance model.

## Permanent status

**Status: PERMANENT STRATEGIC PILLAR / ACTIVE RESEARCH.**

The ambition is broad, but no universal mineral replacement is currently demonstrated. Each specific claim must advance through evidence gates and may conclude that direct carbon substitution is unsuitable for a given function. The project publishes those limits rather than hiding them.
