# UQPU Open Research Community

## An open invitation

The **UQPU — Universal Quantum Processing Unit** project is open to researchers, engineers, students, quantum-computing practitioners, GPU/HPC specialists, compiler developers, physicists, mathematicians, AI researchers and anyone interested in exploring the future of computing.

The central question is ambitious:

> Can a newly designed quantum-first processor eventually achieve every major functional goal currently served by GPUs, without needing to reproduce the GPU's internal computational process?

We welcome both supporting and critical research.

## Ways to participate

### Research
Propose quantum-native replacements for GPU workload classes and analyze their computational complexity.

### Engineering
Build compiler components, simulators, resource estimators, benchmark harnesses and experimental backends.

### Quantum hardware
Explore superconducting, trapped-ion, neutral-atom, photonic, continuous-variable, analog, annealing and future modalities.

### GPU/HPC expertise
Provide realistic GPU baselines and identify architectural assumptions that quantum proposals must beat.

### Economics
Improve total-cost-of-computation models covering CAPEX, energy, cooling, QEC, control systems, utilization, lifetime and maintenance.

### Critical analysis
Find lower bounds, bottlenecks, impossible assumptions or workload classes where quantum replacement is unlikely to be economically useful.

Critical results are explicitly welcome.

## How to contribute

Public contributors can **Fork → create a branch → submit a Pull Request**.

Issues can be used for concrete research questions, experiments, bugs and benchmark proposals.

GitHub Discussions, when enabled for this repository, can be used for broader ideas, questions and architecture conversations.

## Project philosophy

The goal is not to prove a predetermined conclusion.

The goal is to determine, through theory, simulation, resource estimation and eventually hardware experiments, where UQPU functional and economic replacement of GPU workloads is possible — and where it is not.
