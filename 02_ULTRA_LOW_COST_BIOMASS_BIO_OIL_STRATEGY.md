# 02 — Ultra-Low-Cost Biomass Bio-Oil / Biofuel Strategy

**Permanent strategic pillar:** INV-031  
**Primary lanes:** E + F + H, integrated with D/G and the full A–H program  
**Evidence status:** ACTIVE RESEARCH / NOT_YET_DEMONSTRATED_AT_TARGET_COST  
**Date:** 2026-09-10

# Highest-priority objective

Research how to make useful liquid fuels and refinery-compatible biomass intermediates **as inexpensive as scientifically, energetically and economically possible**, using Pangola grass and other abundant agricultural residues as candidate feedstocks.

This is not merely a fuel side project. Cheap liquid energy affects agriculture, logistics, mining, construction, factories, semiconductor/quantum manufacturing, backup power, aviation/shipping where liquid fuel remains useful, and the financing burden of the wider Ketskaew Chulamani industrial plan.

The correct metric is **cost per accepted delivered energy/service unit**, not the raw price of wet biomass or crude pyrolysis liquid.

## Candidate technical pathways

The research program keeps multiple pathways alive until evidence eliminates them:

1. fast pyrolysis -> raw bio-oil -> stabilization/upgrading;
2. catalytic fast pyrolysis -> upgraded oxygen-reduced liquid intermediates;
3. hydrothermal liquefaction for sufficiently wet feedstocks where drying penalties would dominate;
4. gasification/syngas-derived liquids where the system economics are superior;
5. refinery co-processing of biomass-derived intermediates to reduce duplicated capital infrastructure;
6. distributed low-cost preprocessing near farms plus centralized upgrading where logistics support it;
7. integrated production of bio-oil + char/carbon materials + heat/gas/coproduction so that each stream carries only its justified share of system cost.

No pathway is assumed to win in advance.

## Mandatory cost equation

For a saleable liquid-energy product, every experiment must eventually populate:

```text
annual saleable liquid = dry_feedstock_per_year × saleable_liquid_yield

levelized liquid cost =
  (annualized plant CAPEX
   + feedstock
   + collection / drying / preprocessing
   + hydrogen / catalyst / upgrading
   + utilities / electricity / heat
   + labor / maintenance / water / waste
   + storage / transport / distribution
   + financing / insurance / compliance
   - defensible coproduct credits)
  / annual accepted saleable liquid or energy output
```

The denominator must respect product quality. A liter of unstable high-oxygen crude pyrolysis oil is not automatically equivalent to a liter of diesel, gasoline, jet fuel or refinery feedstock.

## Current evidence from public research

NREL techno-economic work on fast pyrolysis and bio-oil upgrading demonstrates that **feedstock cost, conversion yield, hydrogen/upgrading configuration and plant capital** materially change the resulting fuel economics. Historical numerical values from older studies are useful sensitivity evidence but must not be presented as current Pangola production costs.

NREL also maintains conversion TEA model families for catalytic fast pyrolysis and other biomass pathways, and public NREL/PNNL co-processing models explicitly investigate whether use of existing refinery infrastructure can reduce biorefinery capital and production cost.

A 2019 NREL-led catalytic fast-pyrolysis result reported improved carbon efficiency and lower calculated blendstock cost from catalyst/reactor changes, illustrating an important project principle: an apparently more expensive component can reduce total fuel cost if it improves yield, lifetime or process intensity enough.

## Pangola-specific evidence gap

The project does **not** yet have a measured Pangola-specific fuel-cost certificate. Required data include at minimum:

- delivered dry feedstock cost and seasonal availability;
- moisture, ash and relevant inorganic/alkali content;
- preprocessing energy;
- liquid, char and gas yields across controlled conversion conditions;
- liquid water/oxygen content, heating value, acidity, stability and contaminants;
- catalyst lifetime/deactivation where used;
- hydrogen demand for upgrading where used;
- saleable-fuel yield after upgrading, not only crude-oil yield;
- useful value of char/carbon coproducts under the separate INV-030 functional-unit rules;
- plant scale, uptime, CAPEX/OPEX, logistics and financing assumptions.

## Cost-reduction research levers

Priority levers are feedstock logistics, moisture avoidance, higher saleable-carbon yield, lower hydrogen requirement, cheaper long-life catalysts, heat integration, modular manufacturing, plant uptime, co-processing, valuable but non-double-counted coproducts, automation, low-cost electricity, shorter construction time, and lower financing burden.

The program must also test negative results. If Pangola is a poor direct feedstock for a specific liquid-fuel pathway because of ash, moisture, composition or logistics, the result is retained and alternative residues/blends/processes are evaluated.

## Relation to INV-030 carbon materials

Bio-oil and carbon-material research should be integrated rather than forced into competition. Thermochemical conversion can produce both liquid and solid streams, but **coproduct accounting must prevent double counting**. A high-value carbon product may lower allocated fuel cost only when its market, quality, quantity and saleability are defensible.

## Relation to financing and interest

Lower fuel-production CAPEX and OPEX can directly reduce the amount of debt/working capital required by project companies and therefore reduce **absolute interest expense** at a given financing rate. Stable low-cost fuel can also improve cash-flow resilience and may reduce risk premiums if lenders/investors recognize the lower risk. It does not mechanically set central-bank or economy-wide interest rates; that broader channel is conditional and is governed by INV-033.

## Falsification gates

A claim of “cheaper bio-oil/biofuel” requires the same accepted fuel/service function, dated input prices, physical yields, full process energy, capex/opex, financing, coproduct treatment and uncertainty analysis. A target is rejected for a given pathway when physically credible lower-bound costs remain above the competitive incumbent after comparable taxes/subsidies/system boundaries are normalized.

## Near-term experiments

1. Build a Pangola/residue feedstock assay dataset.
2. Run controlled conversion-temperature/residence-time experiments and close mass/energy balances.
3. Measure crude liquid quality rather than yield alone.
4. Model stabilization/upgrading and hydrogen demand.
5. Compare standalone upgrading versus refinery co-processing.
6. Couple the best measured pathway to the project's financing model and INV-033.
