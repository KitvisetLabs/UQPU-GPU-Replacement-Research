# 04 — Interest-Cost and R&D-Finance Compression Strategy

**Permanent strategic pillar:** INV-033  
**Primary lanes:** F + H, integrated with E/D/G/A–C  
**Evidence status:** ACTIVE ECONOMIC MODEL / PROJECT-LEVEL CAUSAL CHANNEL VALID; MACRO INTEREST-RATE EFFECT CONDITIONAL  
**Date:** 2026-09-10

# Highest-priority objective

Make the **cost of financing future research, factories and infrastructure as low as defensibly possible** by reducing the underlying amount of capital that must be borrowed, shortening the time capital remains tied up, reducing technical/commercial risk, and lowering recurring operating-energy/fuel/material burdens.

This is a central Ketskaew Chulamani development principle:

```text
cheaper technology + cheaper materials + cheaper bio-oil/fuel + cheaper electricity
-> lower CAPEX / OPEX / working-capital need
-> smaller required financing principal and/or shorter financing duration
-> lower absolute interest expense at a given interest rate
-> stronger cash flow / debt-service coverage / lower project risk
-> potentially lower project risk premium and WACC if financiers recognize the risk reduction
-> more R&D and infrastructure can be financed per unit of capital
```

The first four arrows are direct project-finance/accounting mechanisms when the relevant costs are actually financed. The later effect on **interest rate/WACC** is conditional on lender/investor behavior and wider macroeconomic conditions.

# Critical scientific/economic boundary

The repository must **not** claim that making fusion electricity, bio-oil or technology cheaper automatically lowers the central-bank policy rate or economy-wide market interest rates.

Economy-wide rates also depend on inflation, monetary policy, sovereign risk, currency risk, credit conditions, savings/investment balance, regulation and financial-market structure.

What the project can directly control is:

- required debt principal;
- project construction duration;
- working-capital requirement;
- technical and completion risk;
- operating-cost volatility;
- revenue/quality reliability;
- asset lifetime and utilization;
- evidence quality and probability of failure;
- financing structure and staged capital exposure.

These can reduce **absolute interest expense** and may reduce risk premiums even if the economy-wide base rate is unchanged.

## Mandatory project-finance metrics

Every major future-industry workstream should eventually record:

```text
principal financed
annual interest / WACC assumption
loan tenor and repayment profile
construction time before revenue
total interest paid
interest during construction
working-capital financing
CAPEX per validated capability
OPEX per accepted useful output
capital at risk per evidence milestone
time-to-evidence
probability-weighted loss / failure exposure
```

For an amortizing fixed-rate loan, total interest can be calculated from the payment stream. At the same rate and tenor, lowering the principal mechanically lowers absolute interest paid.

For capital-intensive energy technologies, financing conditions also materially affect levelized energy cost. IRENA explicitly identifies cost of capital as a major determinant of renewable-electricity prices. Its 2023 financing study gives an illustrative result that a representative solar/wind electricity cost can rise by about 80% when cost of capital is 10% rather than 2% under the stated assumptions. This does not transfer numerically to fusion without a separate model, but it demonstrates why financing belongs inside the engineering objective.

## Two-way feedback loop

The project should research a reinforcing but evidence-gated loop:

```text
R&D lowers technology/material/energy/fuel cost
-> projects need less capital and become less risky
-> financing burden falls
-> more cash is available for R&D and manufacturing scale-up
-> learning curves, automation and scale may lower cost further
```

This loop can also run in reverse. Cost overruns, long construction times, low reliability or uncertain markets raise financing requirements and can make nominally promising technologies uneconomic. Negative feedback must be measured rather than hidden.

## Why bio-oil matters to financing

Low-cost biomass-derived liquid fuels can reduce logistics, agriculture, construction, mining and backup-energy operating expenses where liquid fuels are still needed. If these costs are financed through working capital or project debt, lower recurring spend reduces financing needs. Stable domestic biomass supply may also reduce some commodity-price exposure, but this must be demonstrated with actual supply contracts, storage costs and seasonality data.

## Why fusion electricity matters to financing

Very low-cost reliable electricity, if commercially achieved, could lower operating costs across compute, materials processing, hydrogen production, factories, water systems and infrastructure. However fusion plants themselves may be highly capital-intensive, so a cheap-electricity result requires aggressive control of plant CAPEX, construction time, availability and WACC. A reactor that is physically successful but financially expensive does not close this loop.

## R&D capital-allocation rule

Capital should be released in stages tied to evidence:

```text
CONCEPT / THEORY
-> MODEL_ONLY / SIMULATION
-> component experiment
-> reproducible laboratory evidence
-> prototype
-> pilot
-> production evidence
-> scaled deployment
```

The amount of irreversible capital committed should generally increase only as uncertainty falls. Software, simulation and low-cost experiments are preferred for falsifying weak ideas before expensive fabrication or plant construction when they can answer the same question.

This does not mean hardware, materials or fusion experiments are postponed indefinitely. It means each expensive step should have a defined uncertainty it is buying down.

## Core optimization targets

The program should optimize not only `THB per product`, but also:

- **THB of financed capital per validated research result**;
- **THB interest paid per validated research result**;
- **THB capital at risk per falsified hypothesis**;
- **time from capital deployment to usable evidence**;
- **total lifecycle financing cost per accepted useful service**.

These measures connect scientific productivity directly to the long-run financing capacity of the Ketskaew Chulamani plan.

## Thailand / Southeast Asia context

The IEA Cost of Capital Observatory reported survey ranges for utility-scale solar in Thailand around **6–8% nominal post-tax local-currency WACC for 2024**, compared with lower ranges in many advanced economies. This is contextual evidence only; it is not a quote for this project, fusion plants or biomass projects. Actual financing must be measured from real counterparties and terms.

## Near-term research gates

1. Implement reproducible amortizing-debt and capital-recovery-factor calculations in the UQPU research software.
2. Link bio-oil cost and fusion LCOE sensitivity to financing rate and construction/capital requirements.
3. Add `interest expense per accepted useful result` to long-horizon economic scorekeeping.
4. Separate base interest rate, technology/project risk premium, country/currency premium and financing-structure effects.
5. Quantify how much each cost-reduction program reduces **principal required**, not merely product price.
6. Preserve negative cases where cheaper technology requires so much extra CAPEX or risk that financing cost rises overall.
