## Objective

Test whether an AI-proposed experiment plan improves accepted, held-out model discrimination per total resource budget compared with a simple information-gain/optimal-design baseline. This is an experiment-planning benchmark, not a physical-law discovery claim and not authorization to operate a real instrument or quantum provider.

## Frozen benchmark fixture

Prepare a small versioned suite containing:
1. known governing equations with explicit units and domains;
2. noisy synthetic observations with a preregistered noise model;
3. a held-out parameter or domain regime;
4. non-identifiable cases where distinct equations agree on all training observations but diverge on held-out inputs;
5. hard feasibility constraints and at least one deliberately invalid candidate plan;
6. a negative control that proposes an experiment optimized against a deliberately misspecified simulator.

Record immutable hashes for equations, generated data, splits, noise seeds, simulator versions, constraint sets and scoring code before running any generator.

## Matched comparison

Compare three planners:
- a fixed human-authored baseline plan;
- a simple information-gain / optimal-design baseline;
- an AI-proposed plan using the same typed contract.

Match available simulator calls, candidate-evaluation count, wall-clock budget, CPU/GPU time and energy where measurable. Count generator inference/training allocation when it is part of the tested workflow. Do not compare one planner's prediction quality with another planner's resource price or budget.

## Required candidate-plan schema

Each plan must state:
- incumbent model and its assumptions;
- alternative model/equation and the exact changed assumption;
- observable with units, predicted distributions and uncertainty under each model;
- acceptance predicate and an explicit falsifier;
- held-out prediction and the expected outcome that discriminates models;
- simulator/instrument capability constraints and hard safety limits;
- data provenance, immutable hashes and model versions;
- resource budget for generation, simulation, host work, I/O/transfer, retries, reconstruction and independent replication.

The generator may propose candidates. Deterministic validators and independent adjudication control acceptance. A plan that violates a hard constraint is rejected regardless of its predicted information gain.

## Acceptance metrics

Report, separately:
- fraction of plans satisfying type/unit/domain and hard constraints;
- held-out model-discrimination accuracy and calibration;
- false-discovery rate on non-identifiable cases;
- counterexample detection rate;
- stability across seeds and independent replays;
- accepted discriminating results per total simulator call, wall time, compute time and energy where measurable;
- failed runs, retries, reconstruction and independent replication cost.

Raw symbolic fit, a high self-reported confidence score or an AI-generated equation is never sufficient for acceptance.

## Falsifier and stop conditions

The practical-improvement hypothesis is rejected if the AI planner does not outperform the simple baseline on accepted held-out model discrimination under matched budgets, or if the apparent gain disappears after generator/evaluator compute, failures, I/O, reconstruction and independent replication are charged.

Stop if any plan requires unapproved instrument access, provider credentials, paid QPU usage, hazardous procedures or a violation of the fixed safety/authorization boundary. No such access is needed for this offline benchmark.
