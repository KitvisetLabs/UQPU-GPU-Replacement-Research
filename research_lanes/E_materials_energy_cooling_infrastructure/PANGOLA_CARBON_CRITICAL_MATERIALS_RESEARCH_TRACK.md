# Lane E — Pangola Carbon / Critical-Material Research Track

**Parent strategy:** `01_PANGOLA_BIOMASS_CARBON_TECHNOLOGY_STRATEGY.md`  
**Permanent invariant:** INV-030  
**Evidence status:** ACTIVE RESEARCH / NOT_YET_UNIVERSALLY_DEMONSTRATED

## Research objective

Develop reproducible pathways from Pangola grass and agricultural residues to engineered carbon materials, then test those materials against specific high-value technology-material functions.

## Workstreams

### E-CARBON-01 — Feedstock and carbon precursor control
Measure feedstock moisture, ash, elemental composition, inorganic contaminants, volatile matter and carbon yield. Compare Pangola with rice husk/straw, bagasse, cassava/corn residues, coconut/palm, bamboo, wood and lignin-rich waste.

### E-CARBON-02 — Thermochemical / bio-oil-linked carbon streams
Map pyrolysis/thermochemical conditions producing bio-oil, process gas and solid char. Characterize carbonaceous solids and any useful residues/co-products from bio-oil upgrading/distillation/refining rather than assuming all such streams have equivalent properties.

### E-CARBON-03 — Purification and materials engineering
Test de-ashing, washing, activation, graphitization, heteroatom doping, pore engineering, particle-size control, films/coatings, fibers, composites and nanostructuring. Track process energy, reagent recovery and contamination.

### E-CARBON-04 — Au/Cu/Ag function challenge
Target electrical conduction, contacts, printed conductors, EMI shielding and thermal/electrical interfaces. Compare resistivity, contact resistance, current density, reliability, corrosion/oxidation behavior, process compatibility and cost per functional unit. Direct replacement is accepted only for functions that pass these gates.

### E-CARBON-05 — Li/Co/Ni battery-material dependence reduction
Research biomass hard/porous carbon electrodes, conductive scaffolds, alternative battery chemistries, lower critical-metal loading and recycling/recovery. Do not describe carbon as replacing the Li ion in lithium-ion chemistry. Compare energy density, power, cycle life, safety, temperature behavior and lifecycle cost.

### E-CARBON-06 — Rare-earth dependence reduction
Investigate architecture-level rare-earth elimination/minimization, carbon-enabled thermal/structural redesign, recovery sorbents/membranes and alternative actuator/motor/magnet systems. Do not assume direct carbon equivalence to high-performance rare-earth magnetic/optical functions.

### E-CARBON-07 — Precious/critical-material recovery
Engineer activated/functionalized carbon, membranes and sorbents to recover Au, Cu, Ag, Li, Co, Ni, rare earths and other valuable elements already present in e-waste, industrial wastewater and process streams. Measure selectivity, capacity, regeneration, purity, recovery yield and economics.

### E-CARBON-08 — Semiconductor / quantum / photonic packaging
Test carbon-based thermal spreading, EMI shielding, structural composites, conductive coatings and contamination-compatible materials. Integrate requirements from Lane D and manufacturing constraints from Lane G.

### E-CARBON-09 — Data-center-to-phone material compression
Translate INV-029 into material budgets: mass, volume, thermal conductance, electrical conductance, energy storage, shielding, reliability, critical-mineral grams/device and cost/device. Prioritize carbon routes that simultaneously reduce cost, mass, volume and supply-chain risk.

## Acceptance rule

A material does not count as a replacement merely because it is carbon-rich, inexpensive or renewable. It must meet the required function under defined reliability and manufacturing conditions at a defensible total cost. A negative direct-substitution result must be retained and redirected toward minimization, recovery, hybridization or architecture redesign.
