# BIO-001 contract — one-step vs two-step supercritical-water liquefaction

**Stable ID:** BIO-001-SCWL-TWO-STEP-001  
**Primary owner:** Lane E — biomass conversion/process systems  
**Interfaces:** D/G (catalyst/material/process equipment), F (mass-energy economics), H (industrial deployment)  
**Opened:** 2026-10-30  
**Status:** OPEN / LITERATURE_ONLY / PANGOLA_EXPERIMENT_NOT_RUN  
**Source:** *Energy & Fuels* (2026), DOI 10.1021/acs.energyfuels.6c01592, https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.energyfuels.6c01592  
**Related:** INV-030/031; BIO-001; FIN-001.

## Objective

Determine whether a two-step hydrothermal route (subcritical-water pretreatment followed by supercritical-water liquefaction) improves the final accepted liquid-fuel function per unit of dry Pangola feedstock and total lifecycle cost relative to a matched one-step route.

## Source boundary

The 2026 *Energy & Fuels* paper compares one-step and two-step SCWL across biomass grouped by lipid, carbohydrate and protein composition; the two-step sequence uses a 30-minute subcritical pretreatment. Full publisher text/supplement was inaccessible in this cycle. No yield, product quality or economic values are copied from the abstract. This contract is a proposed Pangola experiment, not a source reproduction.

## Matched experimental design

### Feedstock characterization
For each independent Pangola batch, record:
- moisture and dry-matter basis;
- ash and elemental composition (C/H/N/S/O and relevant inorganics);
- extractives, lignin, cellulose and hemicellulose;
- particle-size distribution, bulk density and pretreatment history;
- lot/season/location provenance and sampling uncertainty.

### Arms
- **Control A:** one-step SCWL.
- **Treatment B:** 30-minute subcritical-water pretreatment followed by SCWL.
- **Optional control C:** subcritical pretreatment only, to measure solubilization and energy/water effects without the supercritical stage.

Keep dry ash-free feed basis, reactor geometry, solids loading, water/feed ratio, heating/cooling ramps, temperature/pressure definitions, residence time, catalyst loading, mixing, quench and separation protocol matched or explicitly accounted for. Randomize run order and include independent feedstock batches and reactor replicates.

### Product and mass balance
Quantify:
- organic liquid/biocrude and aqueous-organic fractions;
- aqueous phase dissolved carbon/organics;
- char/solid residue and ash;
- permanent/non-condensable gas and condensables;
- reactor hold-up, sampling losses and unclosed mass.

Report yields on dry feed, dry ash-free feed and carbon-recovery bases; do not mix bases.

### Final accepted liquid function
A crude-liquid yield is not a fuel yield. Measure upgrading response and final accepted liquid properties against a predeclared target specification, including oxygen/nitrogen/sulfur, water, acidity, viscosity, stability, distillation curve, heating value, density and contaminants. Include hydrogen, catalyst make-up/deactivation/regeneration, separation, fractionation, wastewater and product losses.

### Energy and economics
Close purchased and recovered heat/electricity, pumping, drying, feed preparation, water treatment/recycle, hydrogen production/delivery, catalysts, utilities, labor, maintenance, CAPEX, OPEX, logistics, storage, financing and decommissioning as applicable. Coproduct credits must use a separately defined functional unit, accepted market and non-double-counted mass/energy allocation.

## Acceptance gates

1. Full source article/supplement and protocol provenance pinned.
2. Mass closure and carbon closure within predeclared measurement uncertainty.
3. Energy balance includes feedstock preparation, pretreatment, supercritical heating, upgrading, hydrogen, utilities and recycle.
4. Independent batches reproduce the accepted-liquid yield/quality.
5. The two-step route improves accepted liquid-energy per dry feed and/or total lifecycle cost under matched boundaries, with uncertainty intervals.
6. No process is called ultra-low-cost unless CAPEX/OPEX, financing, logistics and accepted fuel specification are included.

## Falsifiers / stop conditions

Reject the two-step route if pretreatment energy/water/catalyst costs erase the accepted-fuel gain; if increased aqueous/char losses reduce carbon recovery; if upgrading/hydrogen requirements increase; if the liquid fails the accepted specification; or if replicate uncertainty spans no meaningful advantage.
