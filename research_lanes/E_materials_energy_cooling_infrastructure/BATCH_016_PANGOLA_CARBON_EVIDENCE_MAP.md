## Research question

Which expensive/critical technology-material functions have a scientifically credible biomass-carbon route today, and which remain hypotheses requiring Pangola-specific experiments?

## Evidence-ranked map

| Target dependency | Function challenged | Best current biomass/carbon route | Current evidence interpretation | Project decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Key literature anchors reviewed

1. **Rare-earth recovery:** *A review of biomass-based adsorption for rare earth elements recovery*, Journal of Rare Earths 43(11), 2025, DOI `10.1016/j.jre.2025.03.006`. The review describes biochar/biomass adsorption mechanisms, surface modification and regeneration. This supports a **recovery** pathway, not carbon-as-rare-earth magnetic/optical equivalence.

2. **Hard carbon / sodium-ion path:** *Next-generation anodes for high-energy and low-cost sodium-ion batteries*, Nature Reviews Materials, 2025. The review identifies hard carbon as the prevailing Na-ion anode class and discusses remaining capacity/tap-density limitations. This is strategically important because a chemistry change can reduce lithium dependence without pretending carbon is lithium.

3. **Biomass carbon in lithium batteries:** *Lithium-ion batteries and prospects for using biomass-derived carbon as electrode material — Overview*, Sustainable Chemistry One World 8 (2025), article 100112, DOI `10.1016/j.scowo.2025.100112`. It supports biomass carbon as an electrode-material platform and discusses critical-mineral reduction, but does not establish universal replacement of Li/Co/Ni.

4. **Specific precious-metal catalyst-function challenge:** *Biomass-derived metal-free heteroatom doped nanostructured carbon electrocatalysts for high-performance rechargeable lithium–air batteries*, Green Chemistry (2024), DOI `10.1039/D4GC02551B`. This literature supports investigating biomass-derived carbon as a metal-free electrocatalyst for specific ORR/OER contexts, not universal replacement of Au/Ag/Pt/Pd uses.

5. **Guardrail on “graphene-like” claims:** *Biomass-derived graphene-like materials as active electrodes for supercapacitor applications: A critical review*, Chemical Engineering Journal (2022), DOI `10.1016/j.cej.2022.137191`. The review reports that many papers labelled graphene-like biomass carbon do not satisfy conventional graphene Raman fingerprints. Therefore this project must not rename carbonized biomass as graphene without structural evidence.

6. **Related grass process evidence, not Pangola evidence:** *Biochar characterization of invasive Pennisetum purpureum grass: effect of pyrolysis temperature*, Biochar 2 (2020), DOI `10.1007/s42773-020-00048-0`, demonstrates temperature-dependent yield/porosity/carbonization behavior for another tropical grass. It is useful for experiment design but **must not be transferred as measured Pangola (Digitaria eriantha) performance**.

7. **Pangola identity/context:** the open review *Pangola grass as forage for ruminant animals* identifies Pangola as *Digitaria eriantha* (syn. *D. decumbens*), DOI `10.1186/2193-1801-2-604`. The initial source search found Pangola agronomic literature but did not establish a Pangola-specific advanced-carbon technology qualification dataset.

## Most important negative finding

The broad biomass-carbon literature is much stronger than the **Pangola-specific advanced-material evidence** presently available to this project. Evidence from coconut shell, lignin, rice residues, Pennisetum/elephant grass or generic biomass cannot automatically be promoted to Pangola performance.

That gap is valuable: it defines the next experimentally falsifiable program rather than encouraging a generic “biomass carbon works” claim.

## Pangola-specific qualification program

The first Pangola material campaign should measure, with provenance:

- dry feedstock composition and ash/mineral profile;
- solid-carbon yield versus processing condition;
- elemental C/H/O/N/S and residual inorganic contamination;
- Raman/XRD structure rather than marketing labels such as “graphene”;
- BET/pore-size distribution when adsorption/electrode function requires it;
- electrical resistivity/conductivity for conductor/additive candidates;
- electrochemical capacity, rate and cycle-life for electrode candidates;
- adsorption capacity/selectivity/regeneration for Au/Cu/Ag/Li/Co/Ni/REE recovery candidates;
- thermal conductivity/interface resistance for heat-management candidates;
- process energy, chemical consumption, purification yield and cost per delivered functional unit.

## Near-term experiment order

1. **Activated/porous-carbon qualification** — lowest conceptual distance from established biomass-carbon literature.
2. **Critical-metal recovery sorbent screening** — especially REE and selected waste-stream metals, because the literature supports biomass/biochar recovery mechanisms.
3. **Hard-carbon/electrode screening** — characterize whether Pangola-derived carbon can meet relevant electrochemical structure/performance requirements.
4. **Conductive additive / EMI / thermal composite screening** — quantify properties before considering any Cu/Ag/Au minimization claim.
5. **High-performance interconnect/contact substitution** — last, because it carries much stricter electrical/reliability requirements and currently lacks Pangola-specific evidence.

## Relation to INV-029 Data-Center-to-Phone

A future phone-class UQPU system cannot hide expensive material, thermal or energy infrastructure outside its boundary. Every successful Pangola-carbon route should therefore report its effect on device mass, volume, power/thermal burden, lifecycle cost, critical-mineral loading and manufacturability—not only material price per kg.

## Evidence boundary

No result in this review demonstrates universal replacement of Au, Cu, Ag, Li, Co, Ni or rare-earth elements by Pangola carbon. The scientifically valid objective is to maximize **functional substitution + minimization + recovery + hybridization + architecture/chemistry elimination** while preserving negative results for functions where carbon is unsuitable.
