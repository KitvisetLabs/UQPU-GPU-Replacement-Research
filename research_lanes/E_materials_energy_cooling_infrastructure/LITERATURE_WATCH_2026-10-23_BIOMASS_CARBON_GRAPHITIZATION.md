## Primary source and reported result

Hegazy et al., “Metal-carbide-assisted graphitization of biomass carbon and its impact on electrical and textural properties,” Biomass and Bioenergy 211 (2026) 109142. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biombioe.2026.109142. Open institutional copy and publication record: https://oulurepo.oulu.fi/handle/10024/61213. The article studies spruce bark, not Pangola, and investigates Fe, B and Mo during hydrothermal carbonization and subsequent graphitization.

The paper reports that Fe/Mo carbide intermediates improve graphitic ordering; its reported electrical conductivity rises from 14 S/cm for the untreated hydrochar to 22 S/cm with Fe and 30 S/cm with Mo, while the Mo-treated sample's O/C ratio falls from 0.49 to 0.03. These values are measurements on the authors' specified spruce-bark samples and processing route only.

## Genuine novelty and limitations

The useful result is a measured process-property relationship for a biomass-derived conductive carbon, including catalyst-mediated graphitization and a reproducible material characterization pathway. It supports investigating biomass carbon as a functional material candidate, but it does not demonstrate equivalence to copper, gold, silver, rare-earth compounds, semiconductor-grade carbon, EMI shields or any UQPU component.

Critical unknowns for transfer include Pangola/residue composition, catalyst consumption and recovery, residual metals, heat-treatment energy, yield, density, porosity, mechanical and thermal properties, contact resistance, long-term stability, process safety, lifecycle cost and performance at the actual target component's operating conditions. Conductivity alone is not a substitution contract. No elemental transmutation is involved or claimed.

## Proposed falsifiable follow-up for INV-030

1. Freeze a function/property specification for one target component before selecting a material, including conductivity/contact resistance, thermal/mechanical limits, geometry, reliability and acceptable contamination.
2. Replicate the paper's spruce-bark route as a positive-method control where lawful and feasible, then test Pangola and named agricultural residues separately; do not transfer the spruce-bark values to Pangola.
3. Record dry-feedstock mass, moisture, catalyst/additive mass, temperature-time profile, atmosphere, process energy, carbon yield, catalyst recovery and residual elemental analysis.
4. Measure four-point conductivity, density/porosity, contact resistance and the application-specific property set with blinded replicates and uncertainty.
5. Compare against a matched incumbent component on accepted function, durability, supply chain, energy, CAPEX/OPEX, recovery/recycling and lifecycle cost. Reject if the target function or reliability gate fails.

Until that gate passes, BIO/material records remain literature-only or MODEL_ONLY. No material replacement, commercial cost, fuel, fusion or phone-compression claim is promoted.
