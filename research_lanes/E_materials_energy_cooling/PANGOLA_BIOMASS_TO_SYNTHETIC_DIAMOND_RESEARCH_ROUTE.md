## Hypothesis

Carbon-rich solids or carbonaceous coproducts arising from Pangola grass and other agricultural-residue bio-oil / thermochemical conversion may, after sufficient purification and precursor conditioning, become feedstock for synthetic-diamond experiments. If suitable diamond can be produced, intentional color-center engineering may provide a bridge into spin sensing, quantum metrology or quantum-information experiments.

This route is attractive because it attempts to connect three existing project pillars:

```text
agricultural residues
-> bio-oil / thermochemical value chain
-> useful carbon coproduct
-> high-value engineered material
-> synthetic diamond
-> controlled microscopic defect/spin states
-> sensing / information / device function
```

The exact end-to-end route is not established and must be treated as a hypothesis.

## Why the route is scientifically plausible enough to test

1. Biomass is an established precursor family for engineered carbons, including porous carbon, hard carbon and carbon nanomaterials.
2. Synthetic diamond is routinely produced from suitable carbon precursors by HPHT and CVD families of processes; hydrocarbon-based routes are established in diamond synthesis research.
3. Diamond color centers such as NV centers are established spin systems used for quantum sensing and related quantum technologies.

These three statements do **not** prove that a particular Pangola-derived char/residue can make high-quality diamond. The missing link is exactly what this research route is designed to test.

## Gate E-DIA-001 — feedstock definition and mass balance

Freeze the exact source stream:

- biomass species and harvest/source;
- moisture and mineral/soil contamination;
- thermochemical process type and conditions;
- whether the carbon stream is char, coke, soot, heavy residue or another defined fraction;
- dry mass in / carbon-rich mass out;
- carbon yield and coproduct allocation.

No sample may be called “Pangola-derived diamond precursor” without traceable provenance.

## Gate E-DIA-002 — chemistry and contamination

Required measurements should include, as available:

- total carbon and fixed carbon;
- ash;
- H/O/N/S;
- Fe, Ni, Co and other transition metals;
- alkali/alkaline-earth contaminants;
- Si/P and other biomass-derived inorganic content;
- moisture/volatile matter;
- optional isotope composition when relevant.

The purification route must report yield loss, chemical inputs, waste streams and energy.

## Gate E-DIA-003 — structural precursor quality

Characterize the carbon before diamond synthesis with appropriate methods such as:

- Raman D/G features;
- XRD;
- microscopy;
- surface area / pore structure where relevant;
- graphitization degree;
- particle size and morphology.

The acceptance criterion depends on the selected HPHT or CVD precursor route; there is no assumption that maximum graphitization is universally optimal.

## Gate E-DIA-004 — diamond synthesis

At least two route families should remain open:

### HPHT

Potential advantages:
- direct use of solid carbon precursor;
- established diamond-growth industrial family.

Required ledger:
- pressure;
- temperature;
- catalyst/solvent if used;
- dwell time;
- seed/substrate;
- input carbon mass;
- recovered diamond mass;
- energy and equipment burden;
- metallic inclusions / contamination.

### CVD

Potential advantages:
- high-purity films/crystals and deliberate defect engineering;
- separation between biomass carbon source and final gas-phase growth chemistry may allow purification upstream.

Required ledger:
- conversion route from biomass carbon to suitable gas precursor if attempted;
- gas purity;
- substrate/seed;
- plasma/thermal conditions;
- growth rate;
- film/crystal quality;
- energy and gas consumption.

## Gate E-DIA-005 — prove that the product is diamond

Minimum evidence must use appropriate independent phase/quality measurements such as Raman plus XRD/electron diffraction or another defensible orthogonal characterization. A hard-looking carbon particle is not sufficient.

Record:
- sp3/diamond evidence;
- graphitic/non-diamond carbon;
- grain size;
- defects;
- impurities;
- yield;
- repeatability.

## Gate E-DIA-006 — defect engineering

Only after diamond-phase acceptance:

- select an explicit color center (NV, SiV or another justified defect);
- specify implantation/doping/annealing or in-growth route;
- measure defect concentration and charge state where relevant;
- characterize optical spectrum;
- demonstrate ODMR or another accepted spin/state readout for spin-technology claims.

## Gate E-DIA-007 — technology metric

For a sensor or quantum-state application, measure the relevant quantity rather than merely showing fluorescence:

- T1 / T2 / T2* as appropriate;
- ODMR contrast/linewidth;
- photon count/readout fidelity;
- magnetic/electric/thermal/strain sensitivity as applicable;
- spatial resolution;
- operating temperature;
- microwave/laser/control power;
- calibration and stability;
- device packaging burden.

## Gate E-DIA-008 — lifecycle economics

Compare against conventional synthetic-diamond feedstocks/processes and against the incumbent technology function.

Count at minimum:

- biomass collection/preparation;
- bio-oil process allocation;
- carbon recovery;
- purification chemicals and waste;
- precursor conditioning;
- HPHT/CVD CAPEX/OPEX;
- electricity/heat;
- yield losses;
- defect creation;
- characterization;
- packaging/control/readout hardware;
- rejected material;
- lifecycle cost per accepted functional unit.

A low-cost biomass input does not guarantee low-cost device-grade diamond if purification and synthesis dominate cost.

## Primary falsifiers

The route should be deprioritized or redirected if reproducible evidence shows any of the following dominates:

- impurity removal destroys economic/material yield;
- feedstock variability prevents stable precursor quality;
- diamond yield/quality is materially worse than conventional carbon routes with no compensating advantage;
- intended color centers cannot be created/read with acceptable coherence or sensitivity;
- total lifecycle energy/cost exceeds the best comparator for the same function;
- the biomass origin provides no measurable performance, cost, supply-chain or carbon-management benefit.

## Non-claims

This route does not claim that bio-oil residues already produce diamond, that biomass carbon automatically lowers diamond cost, that an NV center is an elementary particle, that diamond enables direct quark/dark-matter control, or that any UQPU hardware advantage has been demonstrated.
