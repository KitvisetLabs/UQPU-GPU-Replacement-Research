## Permanent material-search principle

The project is **not restricted to Pangola grass, biomass carbon, diamond, silicon, rare materials, or any single feedstock**.

The research owner directs the project to search continuously for **any material that is abundant, inexpensive, easy to obtain, recyclable, locally producible, or otherwise structurally advantageous**, and then test whether processing, purification, doping, defects, interfaces, geometry, phase changes or hybridization can transform it into a useful technology primitive.

The canonical search chain is:

```text
cheap / abundant / waste / recycled / locally available material
-> provenance + composition
-> purification / separation / conditioning
-> phase / structure / defect / interface engineering
-> measurable difference ΔX
-> preparation + control + retention + readout
-> accepted technology function
-> scale-up / manufacturability
-> lifecycle energy / materials / cost
-> best incumbent comparison
-> falsifier
-> reuse lessons in the next candidate material
```

The search has **no fixed endpoint or closed candidate list**. Failed candidates remain useful because they reveal purification limits, process bottlenecks, defect physics, interfaces, cost floors or transferable manufacturing methods.

## Candidate material universe

Candidate sources may include, without limitation:

- Pangola grass and other agricultural residues;
- biochar, pyrolysis char, coke, soot and carbonaceous refinery/bio-oil coproducts;
- cellulose, lignin and biopolymers;
- graphite, amorphous carbon and carbon black;
- sand/silica, alumina, clays and common oxides;
- iron-rich, aluminum-rich, calcium-rich and magnesium-rich abundant minerals;
- industrial slags, ashes, tailings and benign waste streams when contaminants are controllable;
- recycled glass, metals, polymers and electronic materials;
- seawater/brines and abundant salts where extraction energy and environmental burden are acceptable;
- inexpensive ceramics, polymers, composites and coatings;
- conventional semiconductor materials when they remain the best functional choice;
- any newly identified low-cost material source that survives safety, environmental, processing and evidence gates.

“Cheap feedstock” alone is never sufficient. Purification, processing, yield, energy, equipment, waste handling, reliability, lifetime, control/readout hardware and rejected material must be included.

## Innovation mechanisms

A low-cost material can become technologically valuable by changing its **function**, not merely its commodity identity. Search mechanisms include:

1. purification and impurity control;
2. crystal/phase transformation;
3. controlled defects and vacancies;
4. substitutional/interstitial doping;
5. isotopic engineering;
6. interfaces and heterostructures;
7. porosity and surface-area engineering;
8. nanoscale geometry and confinement;
9. strain, pressure, electric/magnetic fields and thermal history;
10. chemical functionalization;
11. composite/hybrid architectures;
12. optical, electronic, ionic, magnetic, phononic or spin-state engineering;
13. topology/symmetry manipulation where physically justified;
14. recycling/recovery and circular process integration;
15. architecture redesign that eliminates the need for an expensive material entirely.

## Difference-to-technology bridge

The material program connects directly to INV-036:

```text
material processing
-> two or more distinguishable states/responses
-> controllable transition or gradient
-> stable/readable signal
-> useful device/system function
```

Examples can include band-gap differences, resistance states, polarization, spin states, defect levels, optical transitions, ion-storage states, phase changes, thermal conductivity differences, catalytic selectivity or mechanical states. Each candidate must use the correct physical observable and units; the project does not call every difference “energy.”

## Current starting example — biomass carbon to synthetic diamond

Pangola/agricultural-residue carbon -> synthetic diamond -> color-center spin technology remains a **priority starting hypothesis** because it connects existing biomass, carbon-material and elementary-particle/spin research. It is one instance of this larger material-agnostic search strategy, not the only permitted route.

If a cheaper/easier feedstock yields superior diamond or another superior device material, the project should use it. If diamond is not the best functional endpoint, the project should redirect the same feedstock to the most valuable validated function.

## Promotion contract

Every serious material candidate must state:

`source -> abundance/cost evidence -> composition -> purification -> processing -> engineered structure/state -> measurable function -> comparator -> yield -> energy -> equipment -> waste/environment -> lifetime/reliability -> scale-up -> lifecycle cost -> falsifier -> evidence level`.

No candidate advances to a replacement or cost-advantage claim from feedstock price alone.

## Non-claims

This directive does not claim that any cheap material can replace any expensive material, that waste automatically becomes a high-performance device material, that biomass-derived diamond is already economic, or that unlimited imagination removes thermodynamic, chemical, materials, safety, manufacturing or economic constraints.

The permanent commitment is to keep the **candidate space open and expanding indefinitely while keeping evidence requirements strict**.
